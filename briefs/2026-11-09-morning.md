## I. What Happened

### Oil fell 4% on the first trading day after the weekend's strikes. The move followed words from Trump and a deal report that Qatari officials denied, not anything that happened at sea or in the air.
**The price (confirmed).** January Brent opened at **$97.53** (04:30 IST), below Friday's $98.32 settle, never traded above **$98.42**, and was about **$94.1 (−4.3%)** at 20:35 IST after a low of $92.80. The front fell twice as far as the back: the January–December 2027 gap narrowed to **$14.96** ($25.32 on 30 October). The weekend's owned US strike, a third reported hit on Qeshm and the Riyadh tower hit barely registered. Reuters said ship hits and the Houthi claim supported prices in the Asian morning, but the day's high, $98.42, was only $0.10 above Friday's settle.

**What moved it.** The links to the price below were made by the outlets named, not by us.
- **Sunday, Trump to CNN:** new strikes are "highly unlikely… It depends on whether or not they sign a very strong agreement." Reuters, AA and CNBC tie the lower open and the morning slide to this.
- **16:46–16:51 IST, a report from Israel Hayom:** citing two unnamed diplomatic sources, it said Qatar's prime minister was bringing Trump a plan Iran had approved. Iran would reopen Hormuz and stop attacking Gulf shipping; the US would lift its blockade and unfreeze ~$8bn of Iranian money held in Qatar. Iran has "apparently" dropped its demand to control the strait and charge tolls. The nuclear file is left out.
  - **The fall:** Brent fell from about $96.0 to $94.43 between 16:46 and 16:51 IST. Bloomberg ("Oil Falls on Unconfirmed Report That Iran Eased Hormuz Demands") and Capital Brief made the link.
  - **The timing:** the fall began about four minutes before the earliest relay we could time, so the original article or another feed came first.
- **The denial did not reverse it:** a senior Qatari official told Al Jazeera the report was "totally inaccurate" (~18:39 IST, via CoinDesk), and another told Bloomberg reports on its "details" were "false" (~18:45 IST, via TechFlow). Both spoke off the record. Oil made its low after the denials.
- **Physical weakness:** Morgan Stanley says physical Brent now trades *below* futures, and UBS's Staunovo cites "still-elevated flows out of the Middle East" (both via Reuters). Separately, one ship tracker counted 2.59m barrels leaving Hormuz in the 24 hours to 20:01 IST, about five times its 7-day average; that is one source, and no outlet tied it to the price.

**What it is not.** Not a settle: tonight's settle decides ING's "below $94.62" call and our first deployment condition. The new `brent-crude` watch's open leg (above $100) failed; its other leg, a settle above $103.24 by 11 Nov, is ~$9 away.

### Both capitals talked about ending the war, each on its own conditions.
**Washington.**
- **Trump (CNN, on the record):** "I would if necessary"; "38 bridges in our sight that we can knock down in literally one hour". His claim that the third carrier "just arrived" is not borne out: the *Roosevelt* left Singapore on Sunday (The Week; JPost: "heading to the region").
- **Trump (60 Minutes):** on escalating, "I'm not looking to"; on striking Iran again, "My answer is an emphatic yes."
- **Rubio:** Iran's demands are "outrageous and ridiculous"; "I wouldn't say we're on the verge of a breakthrough."
- **Reported (Jerusalem Post, two sources):** Israel and the US prepared a military operation over the weekend; Trump gave no order and gave Vance "a few days" for a deal.

**Tehran (on the record).**
- **President Pezeshkian:** "The Supreme Leader has ordered us to put an end to the situation of 'neither war nor peace' with the United States." This restates the Leader's 27-October directive, which Pezeshkian first relayed on 28 October and which was conditioned on Iran's terms; "with the United States" is the President's wording. Mojtaba Khamenei governs by written statements and has issued none on this since; there is still no video or audio of him (day 255).
- **The foreign ministry:** its two-day Hormuz plan is "the most reasonable" offer, but sanctions and the blockade must be lifted first, and no enriched uranium leaves Iran. Araghchi: "Our 2-day proposal stands… Or choose war, and see our… Warriors rain down hell."

**Mediators.** Turkey's intelligence chief, İbrahim Kalın, says Qatar will host Vance and Iran's security chief, Mohsen Rezaei, in Doha "next week": "If you can get this agreement in the next few days, then there will not be a strike." One official's claim, unconfirmed. China's envoy Zhai Jun was in Tehran.

### The fighting did not stop, and Iran repeated a claim that may involve Oman.
**Night six (reported by Iranian officials; not owned by the US).** On Sunday night, before oil reopened, state media reported four explosions on Qeshm; Tasnim said "a projectile from the American enemy has hit only Qeshm Island", and Hormozgan's deputy governor, Ahmad Nafisi, said no vital infrastructure was damaged. It is the third hit on Qeshm in a week that Iran attributes to the US. No US official owned it, so the new watch (owned strikes on three nights in a row, or two sites in a night) stays at one owned night. It does count toward a run of three nights under the "resume" definition, which needs no ownership (see Deployment).

**The 8-November ruling stands.** No US official said Trump's "We do minor strikes" meant the 2-November strike, so the "US strikes resume" fire is not reversed; Rubio's "we have a right to defend ourselves" names no strike. The risk that he meant 2 November is unchanged.

**At sea.**
- **UKMTO 199-26 (posted Sunday; missed last time):** a tanker caught fire about 8 nautical miles north-west of Khasab on Saturday evening, and its crew was evacuated; no cause was given. It is the first UKMTO report since 1 November whose effect fits an Iranian claim of a mined tanker. It is not a confirmed mine strike.
- **UKMTO 200-26:** an outbound tanker was "struck by an unknown projectile" in the strait; its crew is safe.

**The claim (unverified).** On Monday morning (Reuters, ~09:53 IST) an unnamed Revolutionary Guards navy official said "enemy" navigation aids "on rocky formations in the southern strait" were destroyed. He did not say which rocks. A similar claim, with no location, ran on 23 October (Mehr). The southern side of the strait is Omani water. Oman, the US and UKMTO have said nothing.

### The Houthis claimed they hit Aramco's Abqaiq plant. Riyadh has confirmed only the damaged office tower.
- **The claim:** the Houthi military spokesman claimed four operations on Sunday. They include Riyadh's "financial centre" and "Aramco tanks", and Aramco at Abqaiq, its largest processing plant, with "fires".
- **What Riyadh confirms:** only the damage to the Aramco tower in the financial district. Nothing confirms or denies Abqaiq, which the Houthis also claimed on 29 October.
- **Monday was quieter:** a Riyadh air alert was lifted within minutes; the coalition claims three launch platforms destroyed in Saada and Sanaa since Sunday.
- **Syria (AP, three officials, anonymous):** Syrian advisers are already in the kingdom, ~600 special forces could arrive "within a week", and Damascus has "decided" to join the Saudi-led Mecca pact. Syria's foreign minister denied only a deployment to Yemen.
- **Condemnations:** the GCC, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Jordan, Egypt, Syria and the UAE. We found none from Oman.
- **Markets:** Saudi stocks rose 0.9%; Reuters credits Trump's signal.
