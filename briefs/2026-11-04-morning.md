## I. What Happened

### Trump admitted the strike on Iran and lost Congress, partly over the war, on the same day. The strike window then opened without a strike.
**The admission.** On *Fox & Friends* on Tuesday, Trump confirmed the 2-November strike (on Qeshm Island, per US officials; he did not name it): "They built an anti-aircraft facility… we watched it get built. As soon as it was completed, we hit it last night very nicely." A "minor attack", it "wasn't war". His officials had called it a response to Iranian fire at a US aircraft; he describes a planned hit on a new site. His written pledge not to strike before the midterms is now broken on the record. The Pentagon and Central Command are still silent.

**The vote** (network projections, still moving). Democrats won the House (NBC projects ~258 of 435 seats) and the Senate (54–44, Alaska and Mississippi uncalled). In the AP's exit poll about six in ten voters called the use of force against Iran the "wrong decision".
- **Trump:** "a part of it was Iran"; "I would have done it again" (WSJ); "We're locked and loaded if we need to go, but there may be no reason for it" (FT, which reports he has no immediate plans to strike).
- **The calendar:** the new Congress sits on 3 January. Until then nothing changes in law, and any later curb faces a veto.

**The window.** The New York Times reports, from US officials, that Central Command's short-campaign plan is ready "as early as Wednesday"; Trump has not ordered it. The first night of the window passed with no strike reported, and Iran has not retaliated for Qeshm.

**What it means.** The vote did not end the military option: the new majorities arrive only on 3 January. Markets read the result as peace; neither government has acted as if it were.

### Tehran read the vote as a verdict on the war, and warned against reading it as a reason to concede.
**The Leader.** Mojtaba Khamenei's written 13-Aban message (4 Nov): anyone with "even a slight hope" that US "partisan and factional shifts", or "concessions", will end America's enmity is "gravely mistaken". If attacks are repeated, "its scope will become much wider". (Confirmed text; no audio or video, as always.)

**The negotiators.** Mohsen Rezaei, the security council secretary to whom Vance's letter was addressed, has sent no reply to it that anyone has reported. On 3 November he called "America's response to Iran's rights and demands" "not convincing": Hormuz reopens only if Washington meets all seven conditions. The armed forces chief's list now adds reparations and Iranian "sovereignty" over the strait. Speaker Ghalibaf: "Americans said NO TO WAR… our NO comes with a Nightmare."

**The live channel is Qatar.**
- **On the record:** Doha runs a "real-time exchange of proposals" toward "an interim deal".
- **Reported, not confirmed:** Al Arabiya and Al-Araby say Washington approved the draft (unnamed sources). One official told Reuters feedback is due "within 36 hours", about dawn on 5 November in India.
- **Iran:** the sides are "far from" a deal (a senior official). Rezaei reportedly vetoed a direct US–Iran channel (one Emirati source).

### Oil slid to the edge of $100 but did not cross it. The physical premium is gone; diesel went the other way.
**The price.** January Brent settled at $103.60 on Tuesday (−1.1%) and was ~$102.3 on Wednesday afternoon; February is already below $100.
- **Tuesday:** a ~$6 drop in under two hours to $100.80, in two legs: a headline that the Leader had authorised talks on off-limits issues, then an Al Jazeera report that Washington had approved a draft deal. It reversed when Iran and Qatar walked the report back.
- **Wednesday:** Trump's FT interview (no immediate strike plans, per the FT), and a Democratic Congress read as a brake.
- **Not all Iran:** part of the fall is the Kremlin-confirmed Russia–Ukraine–US talks in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November.

**The physical market (reported).** Bloomberg puts physical North Sea crude (Dated Brent) only ~$3.70 above futures, against ~$30 in early October; a West African cargo sold $25 below it. With the spot premium gone, what holds the front month above bank fair-value estimates (JPMorgan ~$94, Goldman ~$85) is futures risk premium: roughly $8–17 of the ~$102.

**Diesel.** Heating-oil futures rose 2.5% on the day crude fell; the diesel margin widened from ~$81 to ~$87 a barrel, with Gulf product exports at about half of normal (Vortexa). Cheaper crude is not reaching diesel buyers. India's pump prices are frozen, so for India the wider margin shows up in refiners' and state retailers' books, not at the pump.

### Foreign money returned to India in size, and the rupee hit its ceiling and was pushed back.
**The flow.** Foreign investors bought ₹6,836.81 crore of Indian shares on Tuesday (exchange provisional data), reported as their biggest day of 2026: their third straight buying day, on a day they sold ₩1.52trn of Korean shares. This is what the graph's June test for a swap from the AI trade to India looks for, and the trigger **fired** (Section III); three provisional sessions do not yet prove the swap. Caveats: the figures are provisional, domestic funds sold ₹3,679 crore, and talk of capital-gains relief for foreigners is a second driver.

**Markets.**
- **Nifty:** 23,810.85 on Tuesday and 23,960.60 on Wednesday, five straight gains and the highest close since 1 September.
- **IT:** −1.5% on Wednesday (TCS −2.5%), on a US result that local copy says will slow AI spending.
- **Rupee:** touched exactly 94.50 and closed at 94.58 (PTI provisional) as dollar buyers "gathered around 94.50" (Reuters). The dealers' cap held its first test.
- **Bonds:** the 10-year is ~7.23–7.26%, from 7.41% on 30 October.

### The US vote moved markets more than the war did.
- **Overnight:** US tech futures fell as much as 2.4% and the Nikkei almost 5% as results came in, then everything reversed. By 17:00 IST S&P futures were +1.1% and European shares +1.5–1.8%.
- **Gold** rose 2.9% to ~$4,053 mostly because the dollar fell 0.4% (we put the war's share at ~20%).
- **The Fed:** a December hike is ~80–85% priced (our calculation from futures), up from ~73% on Monday, after strong job-openings data. The US 10-year closed at 4.95%, a fourth straight close below 5%.
- **AI:** Meta sold $15bn of bonds to ~4× demand. Then Texas elected (projection) a governor who, per CNBC, backs a data-centre moratorium; Wells Fargo puts 27% of planned US data-centre capacity there. Foreigners sold ₩2.5trn more of Korean shares.
- **Defence:** European defence shares fell 2–5% on a Congress less willing to spend.

### The ship war hit a tanker reportedly on Iran's blacklist; Iran claimed far more than the record shows.
**The hit.** The UK navy's maritime authority (UKMTO) logged two projectiles hitting a tanker's engine room 14 nautical miles off Limah, Oman, on 3 November. Trade sources name it the Hong Kong-owned supertanker *Grit*, reportedly on Iran's blacklist; the crew was safe. It is not a Gulf state-fleet ship, so the graph's enforcement watch has not fired.

**The claims.** On 4 November the Revolutionary Guards claimed four tankers "destroyed" by mines, two vessels "stopped" and a 56th US drone downed. None is named or matches a UKMTO report. Tracked strait crossings are in single digits a day (Windward). An Iranian MP says India is one of four countries "pardoned" for passage, "although we also take money from them" (claimed; no Indian response).

### Israel certified its election, and Trump said US aid to Israel will end.
- **Certified (4 Nov):** Likud and Shas abstained and only Otzma voted against, so the refusal Likud was reported to be weighing did not happen. The opposition backs Eisenkot and has ruled out the Arab parties; no pledged route reaches 61 seats. Herzog consults from tonight.
- **Aid:** Trump said on 3 November that about $4bn a year of US aid ends "by the end of the year", under a "deal" with Netanyahu. Israeli officials were reportedly "surprised" by the timing; Netanyahu himself spoke in January of tapering aid over ten years. The 2016 agreement runs to 2028 and no mechanism was given.
- **Chemical weapons (reported, one NYT origin):** Israel has told the US and Gulf states that Iran may use them. Gulf officials call it "unserious"; some US officials see pressure to restart the war.
- **No fire** on any Israel front; Hezbollah silent while Israeli shelling continued in ~15 Lebanese villages.

### On the Saudi front, fewer launches are attributed, and a land front is opening near Aden.
- **Drones:** the coalition downed three on 3 November ("Makkah region", "Eastern Region", "Southern Region") and attributed only the southern one to the Houthis. Six drones over the interior in five days have had no named launcher. The Houthis denied Makkah, then claimed hits on Abha airport and King Khalid Air Base (unconfirmed).
- **Aden:** Reuters (2 Nov, four military sources; missed in the last brief) puts the Houthis ~80 km from Aden, the closest since 2015, and says the advance could let them outflank government forces on the mountains overlooking the Bab el-Mandeb strait.
- **An Indian death:** an Indian injured in the 10-October Riyadh airport attack has died (Indian Embassy), the third Indian killed by Houthi fire in the kingdom on the graph's count.
- **Aramco** says "preliminary engineering is underway" on expanding the East-West pipeline from 7 to 10 million barrels a day. Its December prices are not out.
