## I. What Happened

### Iran's security chief said "not a single particle" of its enriched uranium will leave the country, and the parliament speaker said Hormuz will "never return" to its pre-war state. Oil rose 1.4% on Wednesday. Over Iran, a third quiet night. Israel's security cabinet meets on Sunday, after a report that the army was told to raise its readiness for a possible strike.
**The words (confirmed: on the record).**
- **Mohsen Rezaei, the security chief, to the Lebanese channel Al-Mayadeen:**
  - "We will not allow even a single particle of enriched nuclear material to leave Iran." "We need enriched materials to design nuclear-powered ships and submarines" (Mehr's English).
  - "If American aircraft take off from an American military base, even one located in a European country… to bomb Iran, we will strike those bases as well" (AFP). Later, on X: "NO SAFE DISTANCE. NO SAFE HAVEN. NO IMMUNITY." Both are tied to a new US war on Iran.
  - He denied any plan to meet Vice-President Vance.
- **Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf, to Al Jazeera:** the strait will "never return" to its pre-war status; "There are no 'free waters'… the 1982 [UN law of the sea convention] does not apply to the strait." "The ball is now in the American court."
  - **Claimed, not confirmed:** an "understanding with Oman on the shipping corridor and traffic separation scheme" (the agreed lanes for ships), and that US officials asked Putin to broker talks. Oman and the Kremlin have said nothing.
- **Not new in substance:** Rezaei made a similar statement on 20 October (a search summary; not opened), and a June report carried the same Hormuz line (JNS headline, 23 June: "Tehran's chief negotiator: Strait of Hormuz won't return to pre-war status"). What is new is the setting: a US talks report on the same day.

**The price (confirmed).**
- **Wednesday's settle:** January Brent settled at **$92.76**, up 1.39% (Reuters, via relays; Kitco).
- **Two sharp rises, both in oil alone** (US share futures flat or lower, gold falling, the dollar flat):
  - **14:44–14:49 IST, $91.58 to $92.71.** It began in the same minute as the first publication of Rezaei's uranium line that we found (Tasnim, in Persian, 14:44 IST). Reuters ties Wednesday's rise to that line, and UBS's Giovanni Staunovo to "fading hopes of an imminent de-escalation".
  - **20:45–20:54 IST, $93.42 to $94.70.** We found nothing published in that window that explains it.
- **Thursday:** **$91.9** at 11:00 IST, down 1%. Reuters (via relays) cites a Russia–Ukraine prisoner-exchange deal, a reported US crude-stock build of ~9 million barrels and "hopes for progress in peace talks". Investing.com cites the report on Vance below.

**Talks (reported).** The Wall Street Journal, citing unnamed US and Middle Eastern officials, says Vance is "considering face-to-face talks" with Rezaei. It says Trump is open to Vance leading and Doha is a possible venue, but "no date set". Vance's spokesman declined to comment.
- **Iran says no:** Rezaei denies any plan to meet Vance. An adviser to Ghalibaf, Mehdi Mohammadi, says Tehran rejected the request (Iran International's paraphrase).
- **Qatar** has said nothing on the record since Tuesday. Ghalibaf says Iran's answer went "through a Qatari mediator".
- **China:** after a Rubio–Wang Yi call, the US readout mentions "any potential trilateral leaders meeting with Russia"; China's does not. Neither mentions Iran.

**Over Iran.** No strike was reported on night nine, the third quiet night running. Iranian state TV announced a planned demolition of old unexploded bombs in southern Isfahan this morning (10:30–11:30 IST), so treat any "Isfahan blasts" report today with care.
- **Trump, at Arlington on Veterans Day:** "We want to make sure we have a total victory, not a 93% victory… It's going to be total." He also said: "hopefully we won't have to destroy any more of that country" (relays differ slightly). He claimed no strike. Rubio's "no need for major combat operations" stands.

**Israel (reported, single origin).** Kan, Israel's public broadcaster, reported late on Wednesday (first relay we found: 23:58 IST; Kan's page was unreadable) that the army was instructed to increase readiness toward a strike in Iran, and that the security cabinet, which must approve such a step, has not met for a month.
- **Ynet:** the cabinet's last decision, on 4 October, "raised readiness for Iran scenarios, including an Israeli strike or an Iranian surprise attack"; Ynet does not say whether that is the order Kan describes. The cabinet meets on **Sunday 15 November** and "may now approve action against Iran".
- **Channel 12:** army chief Zamir again opposes the prime minister's proposed steps, which it says he sees as liable to "ignite another round of fighting". Netanyahu's office: "biased fake news".
- **Gadi Eisenkot,** who holds the mandate to form a government, urged Netanyahu to "exercise restraint and caution against dangerous moves that can wait until a new government", and asked the attorney-general for regular security briefings (JPost; ToI says the letter does not name Iran).
- **Caution:** foreign relays ("Netanyahu orders army to prepare") go further than the Israeli reports. No Israel–Iran fire.

### At sea, a Saudi tanker managed by Bahri, with Indian crew, was in a "possible attack" in the strait. Iran's two navies each claimed mine hits.
- **NCC Fajr (reported):** India's shipping minister said the Saudi-flagged tanker "was involved in a possible attack while transiting the Strait of Hormuz" on Wednesday. All crew are safe, including three Indians. Two ship registries list Bahri's ship-management arm as its manager; Bahri is Saudi Arabia's national shipping company. No attacker has been named, and no statement has come from Bahri or Riyadh.
- **Off Oman (reported, one source):** the security firm Ambrey says a projectile hit a ship's engine room about 7 nautical miles north-east of Lima, at the strait's southern exit, and disabled it. No one was hurt.
- **Claimed:** the Revolutionary Guards' navy says two more tankers hit mines while sailing "under the direction of a US military officer". Iran's regular navy separately says a tanker hit a mine in a "restricted area". Every cause we have from a non-Iranian source this week is a projectile or a drone.
- **This is the second Gulf tanker with Indian crew caught up in an attack or possible attack in two days,** after Tuesday's *Fujairah Energy*, which India said a projectile hit.
- **Oman (reported):** three unnamed sources told Reuters that Iran attacked ship-navigation equipment on Oman's Didamar island. Reuters could not confirm whether anything was destroyed. On Tuesday, an unnamed senior Omani official told Maritime Executive that the aids "had not been struck". Oman has said nothing on the record.

### Saudi Arabia reported seven Houthi missiles and drones in one day, the most in days on our count, all shot down. Missile debris fell in the Mecca region, about 36 km from the Grand Mosque (Sabq).
- **The coalition (reported; the Saudi-led side only):** on Wednesday, three cruise missiles "towards the Riyadh region", two drones towards "the Makkah and southern regions", and two ballistic missiles "in the western region". All were intercepted, with no damage reported.
- **The debris:** Saudi Civil Defence said fragments of two ballistic missiles launched "towards the Makkah region" fell on several sites, including Sabouha village. Sabq, a Saudi outlet, puts it 36 km from the Grand Mosque. Some relays wrote "over Makkah"; Civil Defence did not. The Houthis called the Mecca claim "a new slander".
- **Houthi claims (unconfirmed):** hits on Riyadh's King Khalid airport and on "pumping station No. 11 in the Yanbu area", on the East–West pipeline to the Red Sea. Reuters: Saudi Arabia did not confirm.
- **Near Aden (reported, one source):** a Yemeni government source told Anadolu that two Houthi missiles landed inside the coalition camp at al-Buraiqa.
- **Tadawul,** the Saudi exchange, rose 1.38% on Wednesday, its third straight gain.

### US stocks rose on an AI earnings beat, and then fell after hours on a report about Nvidia's chief. Rates and gold moved on the Fed, not the war.
- **Shares:** the S&P 500 rose **1.31%** and the chip index **5.66%** after CoreWeave's results. After the close, the Wall Street Journal reported that Nvidia's board is preparing to remove chief executive Jensen Huang; the company did not comment. Nvidia fell **8.6%** after hours, and Taiwan's market was down ~2.7% near its close.
- **Rates:** the cash bond market was shut on Wednesday. In Asia on Thursday the ten-year was ~4.99% and the two-year rose to ~4.77% (Bloomberg, via a relay).
- **The Fed (our calculation from futures):** a quarter-point rise on 9 December is fully priced, and a half-point has ~10% odds. Goolsbee of the Chicago Fed called October's inflation "borderline disturbing" (CNBC).
- **Gold** settled at **$4,000.5** and is ~$3,979 today, on a firmer dollar and rate-rise bets (Kitco). It fell during both oil jumps.
- **OPEC's monthly report:** OPEC+ pumped 2.04 million barrels a day more in October. Saudi Arabia told OPEC it produced 10.563 million; OPEC's own secondary-source estimate (via AA, one relay) puts it at ~9.19 million, about 1.4 million lower.
