## I. What Happened

### Saudi Arabia escalated alone in Yemen, and the Houthis answered with mass-casualty strikes on Saudi civilian airports.
- **The airport strikes.** Riyadh's King Khalid airport (KKIA) was attacked on at least five days between 6 and 12 October and hit on four of them (6/7, 8, 10 and 11 October; on 12 October missiles were intercepted over Riyadh, though the Houthis claim a hit). On Saturday 10 October a missile struck terminals 3 and 4, killing 12 people on the spot; an Indian national died of wounds the next day, making 13 dead, including one American. Another 309 were injured, three Indians among them in intensive care. Saudi civil aviation counts **20 dead and 356 injured from 6 to 11 October**, including strikes on the Abha and Dammam airports. It is the deadliest attack on the kingdom in more than a decade. The Houthis claimed the 10 October strike and call the airport campaign retaliation for Saudi strikes on Yemeni civilian infrastructure, including, per Houthi media, Sanaa airport (9–10 October). KKIA is closed until 17 October, and a US aviation warning runs to 10 December.
- **What remains unconfirmed.** The Houthis also claim hits on Aramco plants. AFP saw a fire at the Riyadh refinery on 3 October; the claims on the Shedgum gas plant and Tanajib are uncorroborated. Aramco is silent, and the energy minister says the system is "intact".
- **Riyadh's escalation (it began before the airport strikes and has continued through them).** It did not wait for Washington.
  - Saudi jets have struck Sanaa since 1 October.
  - "Dawn of Yemen", a Yemeni government ground offensive under Saudi oversight, was announced on 4 October and launched on 5 October with about 100 Saudi-led coalition jets in support.
  - The Mecca Pact was formally activated the same day. Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Türkiye agreed to "activate collective deterrence measures"; Pakistani forces are in the kingdom (Pakistan's military spokesman), and Türkiye offers air defence, electronic warfare and technical support but no troops for attacks.
- **The limits so far.** AFP's own government military sources say Perim/Mayun is still in Houthi hands, and the Houthis gained ground around Taiz.
- **Washington's position.** It provides intelligence and targeting but has not struck. Trump said "We may" join (10 October). The Houthis' Bukhaiti promises "a swift and painful response targeting the U.S." if it does.
- **What has not changed.** In 32 days the Houthis have not hit a single non-Saudi merchant ship, and they publicly exempt the Mecca and Medina airports. The Bab el-Mandeb remains Saudi-specific leverage held in reserve.

### The Hormuz ship war intensified and spread, and the IRGC now claims strikes.
- **Scale.** By our tally of UKMTO-numbered incidents (a count, not an official total), about **25 vessels were struck in the 20 days between 23 September and 12 October** — about 1.25 a day, against five in the six days before the last brief (about 0.8 a day). The peak weeks ran at 11–12 attacks (UKMTO; Reuters), roughly double the earlier rate. **Six were hit on Sunday 11 October alone.**
- **Casualties.** An Indian crewman was killed on *Cape Dao* (23 September), 12 crew were injured on *On Peace* (6 October; 11 of them Indian), and two Indians are missing after a drone hit *Petro Dolphin* at Oman's Sohar anchorage (11 October).
- **Spread off the shipping lanes.** Attacks reached Qatari waters (7 October), a UAE anchorage (9 October) and Sohar. Bloomberg reported on 12 October that at least ten supertankers and several Suezmaxes bound for Fujairah had stopped or reversed course in recent days.
- **Attribution has changed.**
  - The IRGC claimed strikes on 9, 10 and 12 October. The one claim that could be checked against an owner (*NV Sunshine*) was denied.
  - **Habibollah Sayyari, chief of staff and joint coordinator of Iran's regular army (Artesh), said on 11 October: "We have struck blows against enemy vessels. If anything else happens against Iran, we will strike them again."** He did not say which vessels, and Press TV ran it as a conditional threat; it is not an admission of any specific merchant-ship strike.
  - The IRGC published its targeting rule: ships on routes Iran has not authorised, ships with transponders off, and ships "cooperating with the US military".
  - JMIC advisories (the US-led maritime centre that publishes through UKMTO), as relayed by gCaptain, describe the campaign as IRGC/Iranian. UKMTO's individual notices still say "unknown projectile", and no flag state, owner or India's MEA has named Iran.
- **Traffic.** September was the busiest month of the war (Lloyd's List: 477-plus non-Iranian transits). Then the count broke: Kpler counted **7 transits on 6 October**; Windward about 12 a day on 9–11 October. Washington says "the Straits are open".

### Oil spent three weeks above $100 — and Saudi Arabia's bypass coming back did not bring it down.
- **The futures market.** November Brent settled at $99.25 on 22 September, the day of the last brief — the lowest settle of the window. The front month then **settled above $100 for 14 straight sessions** and peaked at **$106.65 on Monday 12 October**. It trades at $104.50 today (−2.0%), which wires attribute mainly to Russian supply prospects. Intraday it dipped to about $97 twice: $96.56 on 1 October (the December contract's first day as front month) and $97.08 on 6 October.
- **The Saudi bypass.** The East-West pipeline restarted on 22 September. Saudi Arabia's energy minister says it carried **5.8 mb/d on 6 October** — much faster than Kpler's "half within a month". September Saudi exports were 6.67 mb/d, and Bloomberg reports Europe's November allocations restored.
- **Why the price held anyway.** On 5 October Aramco cut its Asian list price to −$5 (deepest since 2020) to offset record freight and defend market share. A week later Reuters reported that Asian buyers **refrained from chartering ships to load inside Hormuz**, so Aramco moved those volumes into delivered spot sales — some reportedly about **$20 over** the list price. Physical crude tells the same story:
  - Dated Brent ran $25–28 over futures in October (EIA; CommodityScope's indicative price was $134 on 12 October).
  - Russia's Urals traded above Brent.
  - The supertanker benchmark (TD3C) hit an **all-time $1.41m a day**.
- **Other drivers.** Hurricane Isaias shut up to 72% of Gulf of Mexico output (non-war). Each de-escalation headline — the G7's 100mb stock release, Trump's pledge, Russian diesel — took $2–5 off intraday at most, and most of those dips were gone by the settle.

### India: every condition we were watching broke the wrong way — then the RBI moved to defend the rupee directly.
- **The squeeze.**
  - The RBI **raised the repo rate 25bp to 5.50% on 7 October** (6–0) and moved to "calibrated tightening" (4–2), saying rate cuts are "off the table".
  - **September CPI printed 5.54%** (food 7.74%; onions +108%). The RBI projects 6.0% for the third quarter.
  - **Foreign investors sold ₹35,861 crore of equities in September** — through our ₹25,000 crore line — and, per PTI, ₹44,166 crore more by about 9 October. The 2026 total is ₹3.04 lakh crore, a record year.
  - **Reserves fell $46bn in three weeks to $734.6bn**, including the largest weekly fall on record.
  - The 10-year bond yield hit a **three-year high near 7.40%**.
  - The rupee closed at **96.71–96.88 on 7–9 October** — past our 96.60 line, though short of the true record of 96.96 set in May.
  - The Nifty fell to **22,231.80 on 8 October**, its lowest since April 2025 and 1% above our 22,000 trigger.
- **The defence (Saturday 10 October).** The RBI said it will meet **the entire daily dollar needs of IOC, BPCL and HPCL from its reserves**. It also required banks to hold a **20% foreign-exchange risk reserve with the RBI on eligible rupee derivative contracts above $2mn**, banned rebooking of cancelled contracts, and cut the threshold for hedging without proof of exposure from $100mn to $5mn — Bloomberg calls it the strongest action since 2013.
- **The result.** The rupee had its **best day in six months on 12 October (95.78)** and traded around 95.3 today. Today the Nifty rose 1.6% to 22,722, led by IT: HCLTech raised its full-year guidance and TCS beat estimates.
- **What held, and what it costs.** Domestic institutions bought ₹64,759 crore in September, and monthly SIP inflows hit a record. The bill for the rupee floor is paid in reserves.

### Washington and Tehran swapped papers, then Trump put a date on not bombing.
- **The one round.** Witkoff and Kushner held about three hours of **mediated talks with Foreign Minister Araghchi's delegation in New York on 22 September**. Qatari and Pakistani mediators shuttled between the rooms, and both governments confirm the contact. It was the first since June, and there has been no second round. The same day's Trump–GCC meeting published nothing, and the 24 September Trump–Xi summit produced no Chinese commitment on Hormuz.
- **The paper exchange.** Trump rejected Iran's "seven-day plan" on 26 September. Papers have since moved through Qatar, Pakistan and Iraq. Tehran now speaks with two voices:
  - Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council (which the president chairs), said on 11 October that the US reply was "insufficient" and that Iran is "no longer in the process of responding". By his account Washington "accepted some" of Iran's conditions — a claim from one side only.
  - Deputy foreign minister Gharibabadi told Al-Araby TV on Monday 12 October that Iran is "finalizing" its response (Mehr).
- **The gap.** It is still about sequencing. Vance said Iran must make a "meaningful" cut in enrichment capacity (Reuters, 6 October) — read by analysts as a softening from zero enrichment. Iran wants the blockade lifted and its funds released first, and Washington has reportedly rejected Iranian–Omani management of the strait (The National).
- **The pledge.** On 8 October Trump wrote: **"We will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the Midterm Elections… November 3rd."** The blockade stays "in full force". This came a day after The Atlantic, and on the same day as the NYT and Axios, reported that CENTCOM had been told to finish preparing for major combat, including a roughly three-day campaign — reports, not confirmed orders. A second carrier group and about 10,000 sailors and Marines are reported en route (Bloomberg, citing a US official).
- **Inside Iran.**
  - The economic squeeze: Iran loaded **zero crude in September** (Bloomberg tracking), its oil minister resigned, and the rial set a record near 2.72m to the dollar.
  - The politics: hardliners filed an impeachment motion against Araghchi.
  - The leadership: Mojtaba Khamenei is unseen on day 228.

### US rates — not inflation expectations, and not Hormuz — are what is moving money out of Asia; the war explains about a third of the rise.
- **US rates.** The **US 10-year touched 5.35–5.37% on 7 October, the highest since 2002**, and has closed above 5% every session since 23 September. The 10-year breakeven did not move (2.33%), so the whole rise is real yield and term premium.
- **The Fed.** A 28 October hike went from about 73% priced (23 September) to about 17–22% after soft core PCE (30 September) and payrolls of +29k (2 October). December is still about 85%. In other words, the Fed is being priced to look through $105 oil.
- **Gold** fell 3.8% in the war's most violent stretch since July. It now trades inverse to oil: oil up → hike odds up → real yields up → gold down.
- **Korea's chip selloff (non-war).** The **KOSPI fell 5.46% today to 6,035** and is down 13.9% since 22 September. Samsung fell 7.4% and SK hynix 10.1% on reports that OpenAI's revenue is lower than believed (FT) and on China's CXMT memory announcement. AP's widely carried "−7.4%" was the intraday low.
- **Europe.** European gas rose 18% to **€86.65** as Qatar's October LNG exports tracked just 0.6 Mt. EU storage is 73.6%.
- **Correction to the last brief.** The ECB **raised rates 25bp to 2.50% on 10 September**; we wrote that it held. September euro-area inflation was 3.8%.

### The quiet threads held, but their meaning changed.
- **US strikes on Iran.** None on Iranian territory since 1 September. That quiet is now pledged to 3 November. The only US kinetic act was disabling a blockade-runner at sea (10 October).
- **Iranian missiles.** No Iranian salvo at any state for about 34 days; the fire in the window has gone at ships, which the IRGC's own claims and JMIC tie to Iran but UKMTO has not attributed. Bloomberg, Haaretz (citing IDF officials) and ISW report that its missile stockpile is being replenished (reported). Bloomberg also reports Russian missile supplies, which the White House rejects.
- **The nuclear sites.** The firebreak held an eleventh window. The WSJ reports Iran fortifying them, and Axios reports nuclear targets in the US option set.
- **Israel and Iran.** No exchange. Channel 12 reported that US officers told the IDF to prepare for "a massive attack… within three weeks" (single source). Haaretz reports the IDF wants any strike after Israel's **27 October election**. The polls average a Netanyahu bloc of about 51 seats, short of the 61 needed.
- **Lebanon.** Not silent but one-sided: Israel struck 370-plus Hezbollah targets in September; Hezbollah has not fired into Israel (one explosive drone at IDF troops inside Lebanon, no casualties).
- **Iraq.** No second attack on Gulf energy in 33 days.

### The Signal You Might Miss
The story the graph told all summer was that North Asia's AI boom was pulling foreign money out of India. In the last three weeks the AI complex cracked — KOSPI −13.9% since 22 September, the chip ETF (SMH) −6.8% from its peak, a KOSPI sidecar (a five-minute halt to program selling) today — and **the money still did not come to India.** Foreigners sold about ₹80,000 crore of Indian equities from 1 September to about 9 October.

The `nifty-50` reversal trigger shows it plainly: leg one (the AI complex down more than 10%) is met; leg two (FPIs buying India) is not. The binding constraint has moved from Seoul to Washington. A **US 10-year above 5.2%** — driven by term premium, with Fed hike odds actually *falling* — pushed the India–US yield spread to about 194bp in early October, near a 22-year low (about 213bp today, after India's own yields rose). Indian brokers now cite it as a top-three reason for selling. The graph's new edge **`us-10y-yield → fii-flows` (6.5)** encodes this; the older `fed → fii-flows` edge did not move, because Fed odds fell while selling rose.

The RBI's Saturday floor makes this sharper, not softer. It stops the rupee's slide by converting the oil bill directly into reserve drawdown (`rbi → indian-oil-marketing` at 10.0), on top of a ~$200bn short forward book. India's recovery tell is therefore no longer "the AI trade rolls over". It is **the US 10Y back under 5%, with the RBI able to withdraw the oil-company window** — the 2013 stabilisation signal.
