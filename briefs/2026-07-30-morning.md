## I. What Happened

### A four-day US–Iran ceasefire pause collapsed this week — Iran hit a US base in Jordan, the US answered with a two-hour "heavy wave," and the June pattern repeated exactly.
The most important development of the gap is that the off-ramp the 24 July brief flagged as *reported, not signed* did exactly what its predecessor did. The 24 July "breakthroughs" produced a genuine **mutual strike pause from around 25 July** — both sides stopped for four days, Trump saying Iran "asked us very nicely, 'Please stop, let's meet'" — but it was never a signed deal, and Iran's foreign ministry flatly denied any talks were underway, insisting "Tehran will never allow the United States to determine the timing of war and peace" (CNN, NPR, Al Jazeera). Then it broke: on **28 July Iran fired ballistic missiles at a US base in Jordan (Muwaffaq Salti/Azraq) — all intercepted, zero casualties — and on 29 July US Central Command completed a two-hour "heavy wave" on dozens of IRGC targets** (command centres, missile and drone facilities, coastal and maritime sites; explosions at Bandar Abbas and Kish), Trump declaring it "our turn" (CENTCOM, Bloomberg, ABC). This is the June-17 Islamabad memorandum pattern repeating almost to the letter — pause, hope, an Iranian kinetic act, a US restrike — and it vindicates the graph's standing refusal to price reported breakthroughs as resolution. The calibration held on both sides: Iran's strike was telegraphed and intercepted, the US response stayed on military targets.

### The nuclear firebreak held a sixth straight window — through the war's most intense phase and the "heavy wave" itself.
Across roughly two weeks that killed some fifty people in strikes on "critical infrastructure" (Al Jazeera, 18 July) and culminated in the 29 July heavy wave, **no strike has hit Fordow, Natanz, Esfahan, Pickaxe or Bushehr** — the enrichment complex has now survived the most intense fighting of the war untouched, and Iran has matched the restraint with no NPT-withdrawal move and no announced enrichment increase. The firebreak is the war's single most durable feature. Notably, the US war aim also *softened* during the pause: Trump signalled that recovering Iran's enriched uranium is "less urgent" than reopening the Strait and that Iran could keep ballistic missiles "in relative proportion" to its neighbours (House of Commons Library) — reframing the war around the chokepoint, not the bomb. Whether that softening survives the restrike is the question; for now it coexists with maximum kinetic pressure.

### The Fed held but three presidents dissented for a hike — and the Dow's worst day since April 2025 was mostly *not* the war.
The reader's portfolio moved this week on the Fed and on chips far more than on Iran. At the **28–29 July FOMC the Fed held at 3.50–3.75% for a fifth meeting, but on a 9–3 vote with all three dissenters (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) wanting a 25bp hike**; the bond market read it as the Fed "falling behind on inflation," long-dated yields rose, and the **Dow fell 1,153 points (−2.2%), its worst day since April 2025**, with the S&P −1.5% and Nasdaq −1.7% (CNBC, Yahoo Finance). Our attribution of that selloff is roughly half hawkish-Fed, a third a China-driven chip rout, and only about a fifth the Iran restrike — call it ~80% non-war. Markets now price *two* rate hikes in 2026 and none through 2027. The signal for deployment: the US equity drawdown is a monetary-and-chips story that a ceasefire would not repair, and the war-inflation channel is now explicit inside the FOMC.

### Saudi Arabia went to war with the Houthis — coalition strikes in Yemen, and missiles intercepted over its own oil refineries.
The Saudi military response the 24 July brief flagged as merely "mulling" became confirmed action. On **25 July Saudi coalition forces struck Houthi targets in Yemen, after Saudi air defences intercepted two ballistic missiles aimed at oil refineries in Yanbu** (CNBC) — meaning the Houthis did attempt to hit Saudi *production* infrastructure, the higher rung this brief warned of, and Riyadh answered kinetically. US and UK forces also struck Houthi targets, and US and Saudi forces struck Iran-backed militias in Iraq around 29 July (Deccan Herald, Gulf News). The Red Sea and Yemen front is now an openly kinetic second war that stayed hot even during the US–Iran pause — a genuinely separate conflict, not a subplot of the Iran file, and one that keeps a floor under Gulf-shipping risk regardless of what happens with Tehran.

### India is the resilient standout — the Nifty is back above 24,000 and the rupee has recovered — but a hard tariff cliff lands in two days.
A sharp reversal from the "fourth day below 24,000" of a week ago. On **29 July the Nifty rose 1.1% to 24,250 and the Sensex 1.2% to 77,654**, back above 24,000, on rupee appreciation, foreign-investor short-covering and an IT rally off cheap valuations as oil fell ~14% during the pause and the Fed held; the **rupee has recovered to about ₹95.73**, off its ~96.6 record (India TV, market data). The war premium left Indian assets during the pause, and the 29 July restrike — which landed after the Indian close — has not yet cracked the market, which is holding above 24,000 mid-session on the 30th. But the standing risk is now acute and dated: the **hard US reciprocal-tariff cliff is 1 August — two days away — Trump says it "will not be extended," and interim-deal prospects are reported "dim"** (Deccan Herald, AOL). India rallied into a cliff, on a pause that has since broken.

### The KOSPI's 35% July collapse is a China chip shock, not the war — and it has finally killed the helium thesis.
The month's most violent market move has nothing to do with Iran. A **27 July report that China has begun mass-producing domestic immersion DUV lithography machines** — a direct threat to the Samsung/SK Hynix/ASML moat — sent Samsung down 13% and SK Hynix 15% on 28 July, drove the **KOSPI down 33–35% for July (triggering circuit breakers)**, and pulled the US semiconductor ETF down 20% on the month (The Information, CNBC, cryptobriefing). This is a China-competition and AI-valuation event, roughly 95% unrelated to the crisis. It also settles a long-running graph question in the negative: the Korean chip collapse is a *demand and competition* shock, the opposite of a helium *supply* constraint, so the helium→semiconductor cascade the graph has tracked since spring is now falsified and has been cut.

The conditions to re-engage are the same and still unmet: a *signed* US–Iran deal (the breakthroughs collapsed), a resolved trade deal (prospects "dim" at two days out), or FII buying that persists past an oil peak rather than a short-covering pop. **Hold cash through 1 August; the tariff outcome and whether the restrike widens are both resolved within the week and cheap to wait for.**
