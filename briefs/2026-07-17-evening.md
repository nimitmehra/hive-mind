## I. What Happened

### The deal is dead. Iran attacked ships during the funeral, Washington declared the truce over, and the war restarted — exactly along the fault line the graph had flagged.
All three collapse conditions written into the graph on 4 July have now fired. The sequence: on the nights of 6–7 July — at the emotional peak of Ali Khamenei's funeral, and days before the first planned *direct* talks — Iran struck three commercial vessels in Hormuz, including the **Qatari LNG carrier Al Rekayyat** (the first Qatari LNG ship ever hit; Doha blamed Tehran on the record) and the **Saudi supertanker Wedyan** (Saudi FM attribution). The trigger was the unresolved route-and-toll dispute: Washington read the MoU's "safe passage, no charge for 60 days" as an Iranian guarantee; Tehran read it as recognition of its right to manage traffic and charge after day 60 — the ships hit were using the non-Iranian corridor. The US answered with 80+ strikes on 7 July and **Trump declared the truce "over" at the NATO Ankara summit**; the oil-sales license was revoked; and on 14 July deputy foreign minister Gharibabadi declared on state TV that the reimposed blockade had "completely dismantled" the MoU and Iran "currently has no commitments" under it — formal repudiation in substance. Talks never resumed (Baghaei, 15 July: "no plans for negotiations"). Reported but single-chain: Araghchi had told Witkoff and Kushner he could not guarantee the IRGC would stop firing on ships — the message that reportedly tipped Trump toward the hawks (Hegseth, Rubio, Bessent, Ratcliffe) over his own negotiators.

### Hormuz went from 49 transits a day to 3 — this closure is real, and the US blockade is back.
Unlike Iran's June "closure" rhetoric, this one is operationally confirmed: six-plus vessels attacked between 6 and 14 July (cruise-missile strikes on the *Stolt Magnesium*, *Mombasa B* and *Al Bahyah* on 13–14 July; the *GFS Galaxy*'s engine room destroyed on the 11th), **the IMO confirming two seafarer deaths — Indian-casualty reporting identifies two Indian nationals killed in the window** — roughly 6,000 seafarers stranded with evacuations on hold, and the IMO advising against all transit. The **IRGC formally declared the strait closed on 11–12 July** (the war's first formal closure declaration), and the **US naval blockade of Iranian ports took effect 14 July**, with six consecutive nights of US strikes through dawn today — ports, air defenses, the bridges and rail cutting Bandar Abbas off from Tehran, Chabahar, the Abadan refinery. Iran retaliated across **six states' territory**: Bahrain, Kuwait (including confirmed damage to a desalination-and-power plant — the first Iranian hit on Gulf civilian critical infrastructure this war), Qatar (Al Udeid struck twice in eight days; ~63 missiles and 11 drones intercepted overnight), Oman — the mediator — Jordan and Syria. Zero confirmed American casualties anywhere; IRGC damage claims are contradicted on the record by CENTCOM and Qatar. War-risk insurance never softened and now runs **3–10% of hull value ($10–14m per voyage)**, with US/UK/Israel-linked ships priced as "missile magnets." Trump's 20% protection-toll idea was announced and rescinded within 24 hours.

### Oil re-armed 19% to $86 — but that number says "contained enforcement war," while inventories quietly hit five-year lows.
Brent ran from ~$72 (6 July, four-month lows after OPEC+ added another 188k bpd for August) to **$86.09 intraday today** — up 11% this week, the best week since April — yet still far below the February–May peaks of $110–120: the market is pricing an enforcement war with demand destruction and OPEC+ barrels underneath, not a full closure. The discipline check on that complacency is the runway: the IEA has 2026 supply down 3.7 mb/d with surplus returning *only if* strait flows recover, and the **IMF projects global oil inventories at a five-year low (~7.5bn bbl) this month** after drawing ~3.8 mb/d since February. The re-spike is already feeding the Fed: June's cool CPI (3.5% — the cheap-oil month) was dismissed as backward-looking, and under Chair Warsh ("2% is unconditional") the market now prices a **~46.5% chance of a HIKE at the 28–29 July FOMC** and ~73% odds of at least one by December. That chain — war → oil → hike odds → real yields — is why **gold broke $4,000 during an active war**, its largest weekly loss in six weeks: the fourth and most extreme confirmation that this war moves gold *down*, through rates. Diesel is the hidden inflation channel: heating oil +24% on the month with refining cracks at record highs, while US natural gas (−11%) has zero Hormuz exposure.

### Three crises, one tape: the Asian tech crash is NOT the war — and wheat is a different war altogether.
Friday's carnage in Asia — **KOSPI −6.4% (down 23% in a month), Taiwan −6.5%, Nikkei down sharply (print conflict: −2.8% to −5.2%), Shanghai at an 11-month low** — is roughly **two-thirds war-independent**. The anatomy: a DRAM price-fixing class action against Micron, Samsung and SK Hynix (filed 25 June; Micron fell 16% in five sessions from its all-time high), China's CXMT threatening the memory oligopoly, **Beijing's Kimi K3 open-weight model (released Thursday) reviving DeepSeek-style doubts about the US AI-capex arms race**, an ASML-TSMC pricing fight, and a **surprise Bank of Korea rate hike to 2.75% — the first tightening in 3.5 years**. The tell: TSMC posted a blowout quarter (net profit +77%, capex raised to $60–64bn) and was sold anyway; the SOX semiconductor index entered a bear market. The war's genuine contribution is the helium channel (Korea imports 64.7% of its helium from Qatar; fabs are rationing) and the oil-driven BoK hawkishness — real, but minority. Meanwhile **wheat's +14% month is a Russia story, not an Iran story**: the Kerch Strait is closed and 115+ Russian vessels have been struck in the Sea of Azov since 6 July, choking the route for ~a quarter of Russian wheat exports (22% of world trade). China's Q2 GDP miss (4.3%, weakest since 2022) caps the oil premium from the demand side. An investor who bills all of this to Iran will mis-position on any ceasefire.

### India: the stock market absorbed a war; the macro did not.
The Nifty ends the fortnight *above* its 3 July level — **24,334.30 after a +1.1% Friday** — having bought every war gap-down within hours (the 8 July crash of −2.1% was recovered in two sessions on TCS's Q1 beat; the 13 July closure-declaration gap-down of ~600 points was bought back to flat intraday), with India VIX at 12.88, *below* its pre-crash level. That is the DII wall plus a decent earnings season (Infosys, HCLTech, TechM +3% today), not sanguine geopolitics — because the macro is deteriorating everywhere else: the **rupee hit a record low (96.52 close, 96.60 intraday, ~14 July**; 96.30 today after measured RBI intervention; reserves intact at $675bn), June **CPI printed 4.38%** (first above-4% of the new series) with **WPI fuel at +27.4%** — the war fully in the wholesale pipeline — kharif sowing is running −21% on a deficient monsoon, urea has re-inflated 16% in a month (the July 4 subsidy windfall is gone), and the OMCs' margin cushion is burning at $86 Brent with pump prices never cut. The **FII deployment condition fired and failed inside one week**: net buying 6–10 July (₹15,157 cr of July inflows by the 12th — "four months of selling reversed"), then five straight sell days after the closure declaration (~−₹6,950 cr, including −₹4,206 cr Thursday). Foreign flow is now a pure war thermometer. Two Indian seafarers killed by Iranian tanker strikes add a direct diplomatic vector, and the **India-US trade deal remains unsigned 5–7 days from its tariff cliff (22–24 July** — deadline sources conflict), with Russia still supplying ~50% of India's crude in open tension with that framework.

### The containment structure is the real story: every escalation door that could have opened, didn't.
For all the violence, the fortnight's negative findings define the war's shape. **Hezbollah did not fire a single rocket at Israel** after the war restarted — no northern front — and Israel actually conceded "pilot-zone" withdrawals in south Lebanon on 15 July, brokered by a Washington that is visibly firewalling Lebanon from the Iran fight. **Israel has not struck Iran once**; the campaign is purely American, Netanyahu still cannot get a meeting with Trump (Axios: the White House is "disillusioned"), and Israel now heads to an October 27 election with Eisenkot's Yashar polling ahead of Likud. The Houthis sank the *Eternity C* (≥4 dead, 11 crew held) in a 72-hour return, then went quiet — with a reported Iranian instruction to close Bab el-Mandeb held in reserve **if** the US hits Iranian energy infrastructure, which is precisely what Trump has threatened next ("bridges and power plants"). The US is deliberately NOT striking the nuclear complex (Fordow/Natanz/Esfahan untouched; the 440.9 kg of 60% HEU remains unverified — though the Khondab heavy-water plant is out of service per the IAEA). And the off-ramp is being kept warm even mid-campaign: American detainee Dena Karari reportedly released (Reuters chain), Trump saying "Iran wants to negotiate," and the Muscat channel alive — Iran's negotiators visited Oman days after Iran struck Omani territory. Both sides are shooting and signaling for the exit simultaneously; that is the same single strategy this graph has tracked all war, now at higher stakes.
