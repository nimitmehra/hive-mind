## I. What Happened

### The US struck Iran inside the window, on Trump's own account, and he called it "minor". On our rules it is the first qualifying strike: one site, one night, not a campaign.
**The hits (confirmed by Iran).** Two projectiles hit the Suza area of Qeshm Island; the first report came at about 01:08 Tehran time on Saturday 7 November (IRNA, Tasnim). No CENTCOM release followed.

**Trump (on camera, Friday evening in Florida, after every Friday market had closed).** Asked about the Qeshm reports: "I don't want to talk about it. We do minor strikes, but they knock out. They built a little bit of an anti-aircraft device, and we knocked it out in about 15 minutes, maybe five." (Fox's transcription; AP, the Times of Israel and Al Jazeera omit "We do minor strikes".) Fox read this as declining to confirm; AP, Al Jazeera and the Times of Israel as confirming. In the same remarks: "They want to make a deal very badly."

**Why it counts.** No US official says the strike answered Iranian fire. Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed on Friday afternoon, Tehran time, about six hours before the first report of the hits, that their "new advanced air defense system" had shot down a US drone over Qeshm. That is unverified, and it is the only hint that the site fired first. The graph's "US strikes resume" trigger fires on a strike the US owns on the record that is not a single response to fire. Our own note of 4 November said that if a strike inside the window were described the way Trump described the 2-November hit (a site the US "watched" being built and hit "as soon as it was completed"), it would meet that test. The note of 5 November added that if the White House admitted to "ongoing strikes", any owned strike that was not a single response to fire would count, "however small". Friday's "We do minor strikes… They built a little bit of an anti-aircraft device, and we knocked it out" is our reading of both. The kinetic and diplomacy desks read it the other way: a single air-defence hit on Qeshm, of the kind the trigger was written to log. There is a second caveat. Trump named no place or date (CNBC: he "did not explicitly confirm" he meant Qeshm), and his words echo his own 3-November account of the 2-November strike. If the White House says he was describing that strike, the fire is reversed. The fact-checker agreed with the fire, at moderate confidence (see the footer).

**What it is not.** It is not a campaign: one site, one night, no nuclear, energy or leadership target. On the second night (Saturday into Sunday), state TV reported blasts at Qeshm, Jask, Sirik and Bandar Abbas. CENTCOM declined to confirm strikes (AP). Tasnim and state TV tied the sounds to Iran's own forces, but IRNA reported an "impact" on eastern Qeshm and deleted a line confirming one at Jask. If US officials own those, the strikes would cover several sites in one night, a second leg of the trigger.

**The blockade widened.**
- **At sea:** CENTCOM says a US aircraft "fired precision munitions at the stern" of the St Kitts-flagged *Huda* in the Gulf of Oman on 7 November for trying to breach the blockade.
- **On land:** CENTCOM set up "Task Force Iron Gate" to track goods crossing Iran's land borders and the Caspian. Iraq's foreign minister says Washington asked Baghdad to close all but two border crossings with Iran.
- **Pressure:** a $20m reward for information on Iran's nuclear programme.

**What fell away.** The Iraqi airspace closure tied to 7–11 November is refuted. The third carrier, *Roosevelt*, is in Singapore; it reaches the Arabian Sea "late next week" (The National).

### A missile barrage the coalition blames on the Houthis damaged Aramco's tower in Riyadh's financial district.
**The hit (confirmed).** On Sunday morning the Energy Ministry said on the record that an attempt to target an Aramco building in Riyadh caused falling debris and "limited damage", with no injuries (our translation of the Arabic statement). The Houthis had issued no statement by the time of writing. AFP and Reuters witnesses saw blown-out windows across several floors of the Aramco tower in the King Abdullah Financial District.
- **The barrage:** the coalition says it intercepted two ballistic missiles toward Riyadh, one toward the Eastern Province and two cruise missiles. Alerts went out for five areas, including Buqayq (Abqaiq), the site of Aramco's largest processing plant.
- **Context:** AFP calls it the first time the financial district has been targeted in the current escalation. It is not the first damage in Riyadh: the airport was hit on 10 October (13 killed) and an Aramco facility in the city burned on 3 October.
- **What it is not:** an office building, not oil processing or export capacity, so the graph's Aramco trigger does not fire.

**Saturday's claims (unconfirmed).** The Houthis claimed six operations, including the Khurais field and "Pumping Station No. 8" at Dawadmi on the East-West pipeline, Saudi Arabia's route around Hormuz. Riyadh has confirmed none.

**The other way.** Airstrikes near Sanaa killed 33, per the Houthi health ministry (Reuters could not verify); Riyadh says it is "investigating". AFP reports Syrian troops deploying to Saudi Arabia and Yemen; Damascus denies it, and Reuters found only advisers.

**The market.** Tadawul traded ~0.4% lower on Sunday with Aramco's shares flat; Gulf reports blamed waiting on Trump, not Riyadh.

### Iran answered with words: a range limit "lifted", mockery, and talk of an exit.
**The military line (rhetoric).** Revolutionary Guards spokesman Hossein Mohebbi said the 2,000 km limit on missile range "has been lifted for us" and that missile and drone production has moved underground. There was no Iranian missile or drone salvo at any state (~59 days now) and no retaliation announced for Qeshm.

**The political line (on the record).**
- **Mohsen Rezaei** (security council secretary, on X): "You lost the war and the election… Start a war, and you'll face a devastating response."

- **Vance's letter:** still no reply.

**The ship war.** Iran claimed three more tankers mined (about 20 since 1 November, none matched to a UKMTO report). UKMTO logged one unattributed blast at a tanker's waterline off Khasab on 5 November; the crew was safe.

### The deal track grew in the press, but no one went on the record.

- **Personnel:** Stephen Miller replaces Susie Wiles as chief of staff on Monday. The NYT says his pitch was winding the war down; Quincy's Trita Parsi reads the appointment the other way.
