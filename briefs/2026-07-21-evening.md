## I. What Happened

### Three ships were struck in one day — and two of them were inside the corridor America is escorting.
The six-day pause in attacks on shipping ended on 20 July. Note the attribution carefully: UKMTO logged the projectiles as of **unknown** origin, and Iran has not claimed the strikes — the case for Iranian responsibility rests on the corridor targeting, the timing, and Tehran's own framing below. The **Kavomaleas**, a Dynacom-managed products tanker, was hit by two projectiles 8nm off Kumzar, Oman; its crew abandoned ship and it was left adrift and burning. The **Acheloos**, a Dynacom VLCC, was struck the same day. Kuwait's state-owned **Kaifan** reported a drone or missile strike and its crew also abandoned — though the vessel's identification rests on **one named analyst**, so treat the third strike as reported rather than confirmed. The detail that matters: **the first two were struck at approximately the same time, and both were in the US-facilitated southern corridor** — Dynacom being the largest non-Iranian operator still working it. This was a coordinated strike on the American lane and on the operator most conspicuously using it. Iran does not claim the attacks; it claims *consequences*, via Tasnim — vessels on an "unsafe route" that "will certainly face accidents," and a strait "unsafe for the transport of chemical fertiliser or even a single drop of oil and gas." That deniable framing is Tehran asserting a de facto prohibition without owning it. (UKMTO reclassified the Kavomaleas from fire to attack after confirming with the owner; Dynacom, Marisks and Bloomberg AIS corroborate independently.)

### The escorted corridor has effectively been shot out of existence.
Transits ran **8 on 19 July and 4 on Monday** — a five-day average of **12/day against a now-resolved pre-war baseline of ~125** (Clarksons), a 90% collapse and **over 95% in tonnage terms**. But the composition is the real finding. **The visible Omani route has fallen from a 22% share of transits in early July to 2%.** Of 104 crossings since 6 July, **103 carried at least one risk marker** — opaque ownership, dark transit, or Iranian-side routing — and **exactly one was fully conventional.** Half ran with transponders off. Crude leaving the Gulf is down to ~1.5m bpd against 15m pre-war; no mainstream gas carrier has left in ten days, and **no laden Qatari LNG carrier appears to have transited in nearly two weeks** — which is why European gas broke to a four-month high. Ten consecutive nights of American strikes have been justified explicitly as degrading Iran's ability to attack shipping. Ships in the protected lane were hit anyway.

### And yet talks are alive — which is the single fact this brief got wrong two days ago.
Friday's brief recorded "zero talks, zero venue, zero interlocutor." That was wrong by Monday. **Qatar and Pakistan have tabled a 10-day ceasefire** to revive the Islamabad MoU, with a reported core term of returning to pre-9 July positions; Axios says it builds on an Oman-Iran-Qatar plan from Muscat on 11 July. **Iran confirmed receipt on the record** — MFA spokesman Baghaei: mediators "have conveyed proposals to Tehran," and, crucially, *"I reject the duality of either war or diplomacy. Diplomacy is a tool through which we pursue our national interests, just like war."* Washington matched it: Rubio "open to a diplomatic solution," and a US official in writing — strikes continue "until the president deems otherwise, **but talks between our countries are continuing.**" Set against that, Trump's Truth Social directive that Iran will "pay many times over," and **Axios reporting he has not chosen between the ceasefire and a joint US-Israeli campaign — with the decision landing within days.** Araghchi named Iran's price: *"The right time to negotiate is precisely when you have achieved a reliable field and strategic achievement on the military front."* Read literally, the ship attacks are instrumental *to* the negotiation. There is still **no meeting, no venue and no named negotiator** — Day 10 since Muscat.

### The market priced all of that at about one dollar — and volatility *fell*.
Between Friday's close and today: the first US combat deaths were announced, a third American died in Iraq, three ships were struck, and the Houthis declared a blockade of Saudi Arabia. **Brent rose 1.3% Monday and sits at $90.37. The VIX fell 4.8% to 17.76.** The S&P lost 0.19%. Gold, silver, copper and the entire Asian tech complex rallied hard. The market is trading the ceasefire proposal, not the war — and the discipline check confirms it: **Brent is −8.2% over three months** through a declared closure, a naval blockade and ten strike nights. The signal is not in crude but in **products**: Monday's diesel crack settled **$89.77, within 0.6% of the all-time record** set on 16 July, and heating oil is **+27.1% on the month**. Crude is not scarce — roughly 63m barrels float unsold, and June's global inventory build was **entirely oil-on-water while onshore stocks fell ~96mb**. Refined distillate delivered where it is needed is scarce. Watch cracks, not Brent.

### The nuclear tripwire held — but Trump named the mountain.
**Fordow, Natanz and Esfahan remain unstruck.** Iranian media reported explosions at "Isfahan" during night 10; that is the **city**, and neither side reported a strike on the nuclear complex — Iran publicised Darkhovin within hours and has every incentive to publicise this, so its silence is the evidence. What Tehran did instead is the finding: it took the Darkhovin strike **to the UN Security Council**, with its Permanent Representative demanding condemnation and asserting full-scope IAEA safeguards — **invoking the non-proliferation regime in its own favour, the opposite of withdrawal.** But the centre of gravity moved. The WSJ reported that Israel assesses Iran relocated **thousands of centrifuges into "Pickaxe Mountain," 1.5km south of Natanz and 90–137m under rock** — and **Trump named it publicly**: "a possible target for a nice, big, fat shot right near the front door." Discipline matters here: satellite imagery confirms **construction and concealment** over fifteen months; **centrifuge relocation is an Israeli assessment**, offered while Netanyahu presses for resumed strikes. If the material has moved, sparing Fordow and Natanz costs little — which may explain the restraint better than policy does.

### India: the trade deal is going backwards at T−3, and four Indians were killed by a Russian missile.
The direction of travel reversed. Rather than an Indian delegation flying to Washington, **USTR Greer came to Delhi**, and India's **Commerce Secretary went to Geneva** to lead a WTO review that opened today — the senior-most trade bureaucrat on another continent three days before a statutory cliff. Goyal has denied deadlock twice in 72 hours and the US embassy issued a "fake news alert" between the denials. And the domestic veto became physical: **a farmers' Mahapanchayat against the deal took place in Delhi today, the Shambhu border was sealed and farmer leaders detained.** Any case for a signature by Friday has to explain past that. Separately and more gravely, **four Indian seafarers were killed off Odesa on 20 July** and the MEA attributed it to a **Russian missile**, summoning Moscow's envoy. That is four dead in one Black Sea incident against one in the entire Hormuz theatre. **India has now publicly named Tehran and Moscow over harm to its nationals, and has still not named Washington** — while Russian crude runs at record volumes and Washington threatens 100% tariffs over exactly that relationship. India's energy hedge and its trade risk are the same position.

## II. What Analysts Say

### David Schenker — Washington Institute; Assistant Secretary of State for Near Eastern Affairs in Trump's first term
The campaign will not change Iranian behaviour, put as arithmetic: **"The U.S. hit 13,000 targets in the first 6 weeks of the war, and this didn't sway Iran to take a knee."** Ten nights will not do what 13,000 targets could not. **Falsifiable cleanly:** if Iran relaxes its grip on Hormuz — sustained transits recovering toward 60/day — without a negotiated ceasefire, he is wrong. A first-term Trump official conceding the campaign's futility is the strongest bear signal on escalation-as-strategy in this cycle.

### Frank Kendall — former Secretary of the Air Force; the technical rather than political voice
The target set is exhausted: **"the easy targets are pretty much gone. They can hide ballistic missiles and cruise missiles and small air vehicles pretty easily… that's why they're still able to attack not just ships in the Straits, but also our bases in the region."** **The cleanest measurable prediction available:** track whether Iranian launch volume declines over the next 10–14 strike nights. US strikes have expanded to launch sites in northwestern and central Iran; if the tempo holds, Kendall is right.

### Mona Yacoubian — CSIS, Middle East Program
An off-ramp is live: she reads the US response to the Jordan deaths as deliberately **"measured."** Her named downside trigger is specific — **if the Houthi embargo expands into a full Bab el-Mandeb closure, that is "a perfect storm of disruption."** **Falsifiable within days** by whether Trump takes the 10-day proposal or authorises the joint campaign.

### ISW / CTP — Iran Update, 20 July
The Houthi blockade "could result in an expansion of the shipping crisis" — **with the crucial caveat that ISW has recorded no actual Houthi attack on shipping since 7 July**; enforcement is by radio turn-back order only. ISW reads it as partly Houthi self-deterrence against Saudi strikes rather than Iranian direction. **Falsifiable within days:** the first struck Saudi-bound hull converts announcement into blockade.

### Source Tone Assessment
**The most consequential shift is that Iranian media has begun claiming the American dead.** Friday's brief noted that Iran claimed the *base* at Al-Azraq but pointedly not the bodies — a deliberate de-confliction gesture. That is gone: Mehr now runs "Several US troops killed," and Press TV has escalated to "US concealing dozens of casualties." **Whatever restraint operated last week was a decision, and it has been reversed.** In parallel Tehran has **ordered its own media to limit strike-damage coverage** — what a regime does when an argument is going badly. **Washington's Congressional silence broke, but through money, not war powers:** Democrats are blocking the defence bill after the Rules Committee killed an amendment barring funds for attacking Iran, with a House vote tomorrow, while Senate Republicans draft a ~$95bn package including **$60bn to replenish munitions**. **Fox's frame also moved** — from force-generation to chokepoint economics and, tellingly, public opinion; Fox does not run polling stories about a war it wants continued. **Oman remains totally silent for a fifth day** — no ONA statement of any kind — while Axios places the current proposal as building on a plan made in Muscat. That is mediation discipline, and any ONA statement now becomes a first-order signal.

## III. What the Graph Tells Me

### Cascade Watch — beginning with a correction to this brief's own headline call
Thirty-eight nodes moved and **not one trigger escalated**. The most important change is a retraction. Friday's "Signal You Might Miss" argued that Hormuz reopening is gated by **underwriting capacity**, and the graph took `marine-war-risk-insurance→strait-of-hormuz` to weight 10 as "the highest-conviction transmission in the dossier." **It has been cut to 7.0, on three independent legs.** Marsh's own figure **fell** — 3–8% of hull on 28 June to **2–6% of vessel value on 9 July**, same broker, later date, with rates having drifted below 2% before the July attacks. The "six P&I clubs withdrew cover" claim is a **category error**: Lloyd's List established in March that war risk is a separate insurance class excluded from club rulebooks by design, and that episode was resolved within days. And cover is being written — WTW, 9 July: *"terms are available."* The inferred "4–8 week reopening lag" has been retired outright. Elsewhere: **`fed→gold` cut 10→9.0** because the transmission has gone *quiet*, not wrong — Brent at $90 and pump prices at $4.00 moved July hike odds barely, so gold now trades the Fed rather than the war. **`helium→semiconductors` cut to 7.5**: the 8–12 week lag implied fab output cuts should be landing now, and **no producer has announced one.** Three new edges opened: `iran→shipping-tankers` at 9.5, and `houthis→red-sea` and `houthis→saudi-arabia` — the latter deliberately capped, because the Houthi embargo is **a declaration with no enforcement**, and announce-versus-enforce is exactly where the March Houthi error was made.

### The Signal You Might Miss
**The crew market is now pricing Hormuz risk above the insurance market — and it is the binding constraint nobody is quoting.** **Sinokor, the world's largest supertanker owner, is offering seafarers six months' salary for a single ~30-day round trip** — roughly $90,000 for a master against a $15,000 monthly wage, and about triple the industry norm of adding 60 days' pay to a 30-day contract. Set that against the insurance number: at 2–6% of vessel value, a $100m tanker costs up to $6m per transit — real money, but a *quoted, available* price. **The crew premium is the one that is not clearing.** The IMO counts **59 ships attacked and 17 seafarers killed** since February, with ~6,000 still trapped in the Gulf and the evacuation plan frozen for 24 days; India has barred its nationals from these voyages, and Indians are roughly a tenth of the world's seafarers, concentrated in tanker crews. **When labour prices a risk above capital, capital's price is the lagging indicator.** The practical implication inverts Friday's call: reopening is not gated by an underwriting cycle that must grind through weeks of clean data — it is gated by whether masters and crews will sail, which can turn **fast** on a credible ceasefire, and turn back just as fast on one more burning hull.

### Risk Landscape
**Intensifying.** The nuclear file, on rhetoric rather than ordnance — Trump naming Pickaxe Mountain is how target sets get socialised, and Israel is openly waiting for permission ("If he wants us, we'll join"; COGS Zamir: "prepared to immediately resume combat"). And **Jordan**: every Iranian salvo at Aqaba is a near-miss on Eilat, which is now a likelier accidental route into Israeli belligerence than Lebanon.

**Stable, and better than Friday.** The **rial reversed 3.1% off its record** to sit 5.6% from the hyperinflation threshold, with Iranian domestic gold trading at a *discount* to fair value — Iranians de-risking, not panicking. **Hezbollah has fired nothing for fourteen days**, through American deaths, a nuclear-site strike and the start of an Israeli withdrawal it publicly rejects. **Iraqi militias stayed home** — the US death there was an EOD accident, not an attack. And the Lebanon firewall produced its first physical event, though a small one: Israel vacated part of **one** village of three, two of which it never occupied.

**Fading.** Defence as a war hedge — **ITA is −5.6% on the month and did not move on three American deaths or a reported $60bn munitions bill.** Fourth confirmation. Uranium equities likewise: **−17.9% while spot is flat.**

**Deployment (India): defensive, and the near-term risk is now domestic.** The war is not what is moving Indian assets — **bank net-interest-margin compression is**, with HDFC Bank down 7% in two days and ~₹80,000 cr erased despite reported profit growth. The rupee held 96.52, but understand *how*: the RBI has pulled in **$20.7bn through a swap facility**, recouping ~39% of the war's reserve drawdown with **borrowed dollars**, while briefing Reuters that it is debating whether to keep intervening. **That floor is softer than the print.** Watch the **24 July reserves release — the same day as the tariff cliff**; the headline will likely flatter the underlying defence. **Conditions to re-engage are unchanged and now dated:** the trade deal signed (unlikely — the Commerce Secretary is in Geneva and farmers are being detained); FII net buying for three-plus sessions *after* an oil peak (Monday was −₹1,121 cr, absorbed 1.17× by domestic buyers); or a ceasefire that actually convenes with a venue and named negotiators. **The asymmetry to hold: VIX at 17.76 and falling, into a binary that Axios says Trump resolves within days.** Convexity is cheap and the horizon is short.
