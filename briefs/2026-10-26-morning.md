## I. What Happened

### Oil hit a six-month high on a US–Iran round that has not started — while the physical market eased.
**The rally.**
- December Brent settled at **$116.72 on Friday 23 October**, the highest since late April. That is 10.4% above the 13-October level and the 23rd straight settle above $100.
- It came in three jumps:
  - **16 Oct, +3.7%:** a Houthi missile aimed at Yanbu was intercepted, and attacks in Hormuz intensified.
  - **22 Oct, +4.0%:** two were killed at Dammam airport, Aramco evacuated its Dhahran headquarters, and the NYT reported post-election US–Israeli strike plans.
  - **23 Oct, +3.1%:** Washington raised its Saudi travel warning to Level 3, two days after ordering non-essential staff out; the Houthis claimed 48 hours of strikes; Libya cut output.

**The physical market — actual cargoes — eased over the same fortnight:**
- The gap between spot crude and futures shrank from ~$25 to **$6–10** (EIA's spot series is a proxy; there was no Platts/Argus print).
- The Oman–Dubai spread hit its lowest since July, and Russia's Urals swung to a ~$31 discount.
- Trackers put Gulf barrels near pre-war levels: Kpler ~14 mb/d through Hormuz including transfers (82%); Morgan Stanley ~17 mb/d of exports.
- Brent for delivery in December 2027 has not moved (~$86.7).

**What it means.** The market is paying for a dated event — strikes after 3 November, or Houthi damage to Saudi export plants — not for lost barrels.
- **No Saudi barrel is confirmed lost.** The Houthi claims on Yanbu and the East-West pumping stations are unverified, and an Argus source says Yanbu and Shaybah are undamaged.
- Goldman puts the premium at "nearly $25". The tight product is diesel, not crude.
- **Today:** about $115 (−1.3%), after no weekend strike, Trump's "not today" and Qatari mediators in Tehran.

### Washington and Tehran have both tied their next move to the 3 November US elections.
**The pause.** No US strike on Iranian territory has been confirmed since 1 September. The White House restated Trump's no-strike-before-the-midterms pledge on 20 and 22 October. Trump himself swung from "maybe tomorrow" (24 Oct) to "Do I finish it off… or do I make a deal?… Getting to be decision time" (Fox, 25 Oct), then "There will not be a strike today."

**The preparations.** These are *reported*, all on unnamed officials, and none is an order:
- **NYT:** a short US–Israeli campaign after the election against nuclear, energy and missile targets.
- **WSJ:** a carrier rearming with bunker-busters.
- **NBC:** troops quietly moved out of bases in Jordan, Kuwait and Qatar, as before 28 February.
- **CNN:** strikes possible "within 96 hours" of an order.
- **Fox:** a carrier group, amphibious group and Marine unit (~10,000 personnel) are due in the region by the end of November.

**Confirmed.** A memo from the US intelligence chief, first reported by Axios and confirmed by officials to CBS and Fox, says Iranian plots against US personnel are at "advanced stages"; it does not say attacks are imminent. The US cut staff at embassies across the region and ordered non-essential staff out of Saudi Arabia, which State said was "about the Houthis".

**Tehran's mirror image.** Reuters' unnamed Iranian officials say the establishment is "determined not to give Trump a political victory", and that the leadership approved October's ship-war escalation to keep oil high before the vote.
- **No new paper.** Security chief Rezaei's "no longer responding" held; Tehran says the seven-day plan it already handed over is its answer.
- **Araghchi's claim.** The foreign minister claims Washington "accepted" Iran's conditions but wants "a few more clauses". No US official has addressed the claim; Trump said the same evening: "I think we're not negotiating."
- **A disowned offer.** Within hours of an envoy's offer to reopen Hormuz if the blockade is lifted, the IRGC-linked press called a matching Al Arabiya report "lies and fantasy" and said Iran "fundamentally does not accept" a Hormuz-for-blockade trade.

### Saudi Arabia went to Tehran while the Houthis struck its Eastern Province.
**The meeting.** Today Saudi Defence Minister Khalid bin Salman, Pakistan's army chief Munir and Türkiye's intelligence chief Kalin began talks in Tehran with security chief Rezaei's council (Iranian state media). Riyadh said they would also see President Pezeshkian; that meeting was not confirmed by evening.
- It is the first high-level contact between Iran and all three Mecca-pact states since Riyadh entered the war.
- It came 11 days after Riyadh, for the first time, publicly blamed Iran for "encouraging" the Houthis.
- Riyadh calls it an effort to "prevent a resurgence" of escalation. There was no readout by evening.

**The Houthi campaign moved east:**
- **Dhahran:** Aramco's headquarters was hit on 13–14 October ("limited fire").
- **Dammam airport (21 Oct):** two killed, a Saudi and an Indian resident, per Saudi civil aviation; India's embassy confirmed the Indian death.
- **Shaybah oilfield (same day):** debris from an intercepted drone killed two. Riyadh named no attacker and the Houthis have not claimed it.
- **25 Oct:** ten-plus ballistic missiles intercepted in a day.
- **24 Oct:** Saudi civil defence says two missiles "toward the Makkah and Madinah regions" were intercepted, with debris ~20 km from the Grand Mosque. The regions are provinces that also contain Jeddah, Taif, Rabigh and Yanbu. The Houthis deny aiming at the holy cities ("a great and grievous falsehood") and say they targeted oil and military sites, including the Rabigh refinery.
- By our tally of official figures, at least 22 people were killed by Houthi fire in the kingdom in October, 24 including the unclaimed Shaybah drone.

**The cost and the response.**
- Riyadh's airport reopened formally only on 25 October (limited domestic flights from 21 October), and the flagship investment conference is postponed to 2027.
- Riyadh's allies sent defenders, not strikers: British Typhoons, a Greek Patriot battery, and an Egyptian parliamentary vote (result not yet known).
- **Washington still has not struck the Houthis.** The Red Sea stays a Saudi-only war: no attributed attack on any other merchant ship in ~45 days.

### India: the rupee defence is now costing interest rates and bank liquidity, not just reserves.
**The rupee** rallied to a six-week high of **94.38** (21 Oct) on dollars flowing into the record Jio Platforms share sale and on RBI support. It closed at **95.22** today as Brent hit $117. The 96.96 record was never approached.

**The cost:**
- **Reserves** fell a sixth straight week to **$721bn** (PTI), $65bn below the September record.
- **Liquidity.** The RBI's dollar sales, together with tax outflows, a stricter daily cash-reserve rule and its own bond sales, drained the banking system's surplus cash from ₹4.87 lakh crore (30 Sep) to about **₹542 crore** (21 Oct, reported), forcing its first cash injection in three months.
- **Yields.** The 10-year rose to **7.48%**, the highest since March 2023.
- **Rates.** The policy minutes put another hike on the table; HSBC expects 50bp on 4 December.

**Flows and prices.**
- **Foreign investors sold ₹48,056 crore in October to the 23rd**, already more than in September. The pace slowed in the last week, and domestic funds bought ₹59,110 crore.
- Wholesale inflation is 11.4%. Pump prices are frozen at a ~₹20/litre loss on diesel.

**The Nifty was flat (22,753) only because of the split underneath.** IT had its best week since 2008 on results (Infosys's revenue-guidance cut on 23 October was shrugged off). Banks fell 2% and small caps had their worst week since February 2025 (−7.1%).

**People.** An Indian has now been killed on Saudi soil twice this month. The foreign ministry condemned the attacks without naming the attacker, and Air India has suspended Riyadh and Dammam flights to 7 November.

### An AI credit scare, not peace, pushed the US 10-year below 5% — and gold failed as a hedge.
**22 October.**
- Meta's results (released after the close on 21 October) showed free cash flow of −$2.12bn, its first negative quarter in over a decade (per CNBC): capital spending exceeded the cash its operations generated.
- Meta fell 7.0%, chip stocks 6.7%, and the **10-year 17bp to 4.96%** — its first close below 5% since September.
- Most of the fall was in real yields (−12bp; inflation expectations −5bp that day and flat over the fortnight), credit spreads widened, and Brent rose 4% the same day. It was a flight to safety; the yield was back at 5.00% the next day.

**Gold fell below $4,000** (spot ~$3,996, 23 Oct) for the first time since mid-July. It fell even as real yields dropped 16bp — normally gold's cue to rise. Markets sold everything, and a December Fed hike is almost unanimously expected.

**Korea** fell 7.6% on 15 October, with trading halted, and is down 22% in a month. That money did not come to India.

### European gas reached its highest since 2022, €3 short of €100.
TTF (the Dutch gas benchmark) touched **€97.19 today**, +10% since 13 October.
- **Supply:** Qatar's LNG exports are still ~0.6 Mt a month.
- **Competition:** Asian LNG hit a near four-year high ($30.76), pulling cargoes east.
- **Storage:** EU storage has stalled at 74.6%, the lowest for late October since 2009.

On 22 October QatarEnergy signed to *buy* up to 4 Mt a year of US LNG from Cheniere until 2054: the Gulf's biggest exporter is hedging its own damaged plant.

### Hormuz had its worst month of the war — and Iranian officials now claim the campaign as theirs.
**Attacks.** UKMTO's numbered reports (the UK navy's maritime authority) logged 11 ships hit from 13 to 23 October, about one a day, down from 1.25. Only four came in the last week, though reports arrive late. Independent counts make October the worst month, with 28–32 attacks.
- The container ship *GFS Pride* sank on 14 October.
- Attacks reached Saudi waters (including a grenade fired from a small boat), Iraqi waters and the UAE's Gulf of Oman coast.

**Attribution moved, at campaign level.**
- Two senior Iranian officials told Reuters the leadership approved the escalation (reported).
- Rezaei says Iran hits "five or six… every night".
- Lloyd's List Intelligence, an independent shipping-data firm, calls tankers "Iran's most frequently targeted vessel type".
- UKMTO's individual notices still say "unknown projectile". The IRGC's own specific claims are unverified, and Trump calls them "fake hits".

**Indian crews.** No Indian-flagged ship was struck. Two Indian seafarers from *Petro Dolphin* are still missing.

### The quiet threads held.
- **Israel** votes tomorrow, with mainstream polls giving Netanyahu's bloc 48–50 of the 61 seats it needs. There was no Israel–Iran exchange. Reports from the FT, Channel 12 and CNN say Trump and the army chief held back a pre-vote strike; Netanyahu's office calls this "fake news".
- **Iran's missiles:** no salvo at a state for ~47 days, though the Kurdish opposition party that was targeted says Iran fired four ballistic missiles at its base in Iraq on 21 October (Iran has not claimed it).
- **Nuclear sites:** the no-strike line held a 12th window, though the sites are in every reported US target list.
- **Hezbollah** has not fired at Israel.
- **Mojtaba Khamenei** remains unseen on day 241.
- **Uranium:** the gap widened. Rezaei says "not a single gram" of enriched uranium will leave Iran; Washington wants it "disposed". That blocks, for now, Russia's offer to hold it. Pezeshkian meets Putin tomorrow.

### The Signal You Might Miss
India's oil exposure now runs less through the trade deficit than through its own central bank's toolkit.
- **The mechanism.** To hold the rupee the RBI sells dollars, and every dollar sold drains rupees from the banks. The surplus fell from ₹4.87 lakh crore to about ₹542 crore in three weeks; tax outflows and the RBI's own bond sales added to the drain. Bank shares fell 2% and the 10-year rose to 7.48% while US yields *fell* 21bp. The graph's `rbi → nifty-50` and `rbi → indian-oil-marketing` edges are both at 10.0.
- **The tell.** IOC, BPCL and HPCL *rose* 1–4% while crude rose 10% and diesel losses doubled: the market is treating the RBI's dollar window as a guarantee.
- **The asymmetry.** If the post-3-November binary breaks benignly and Brent sheds its ~$20 event premium, the RBI can narrow the window. Liquidity, yields and bank shares would then recover together, faster than the trade arithmetic alone implies. If Brent holds above $115, the RBI faces the 2013 choice between the rupee and rates. The oil companies, priced for rescue, would be the first to reprice.
