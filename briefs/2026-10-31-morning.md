## I. What Happened

### Washington is negotiating and preparing to strike at the same time — and both tracks now point at 4 November.
**The channel.** Vice President Vance wrote to Iran's security chief, Mohsen Rezaei. Qatar delivered the letter on 25 October (NYT) and Iran confirmed receipt on 29 October, but had not replied by Saturday evening.
- **What it reportedly offers:** Vance-led direct talks, the nuclear file "on the table from the outset", and a meeting in Istanbul or Doha (NYT, Washington Post, Axios).
- **Trump's framing:** asked whether he directed Vance to restart talks, he said "No… they need help with medical supplies" (28 Oct), then "if you want to do that, that's OK" (29 Oct).
- **Qatar's push:** its envoys spent 12+ hours in Tehran on 30 October, and Iranian negotiators were in Doha on 31 October. Tehran denied a report that US officials would join them.
- **Tehran's answer:** Araghchi calls the letter "a positive step" but has "no plans for a meeting with American officials". He says Iran's plan could be implemented in "two days", but its conditions have grown: fewer US forces in the region, and, per Arabic-language relays, recognition of the Hormuz route Iran agreed with Oman, with other routes closed. A leaked draft (Al Arabiya, unconfirmed by any party) offers uranium to a third country *or* down-blending.

**The preparation.**
- **Well sourced, not official:** Gulf allies were told to prepare for a "short, intense" US campaign after the midterms, and for Iranian retaliation on energy sites. The WSJ, AP and MS NOW each have it from separate unnamed officials or diplomats; no government has confirmed it.
- **Reported:** it could come "as soon as Nov. 4" (Gulf officials). The CENTCOM commander briefed Trump in person; a Patriot battery is moving from Texas to the region (Israel, per one WSJ relay); 10+ US cargo jets landed at Israeli air bases; and a third carrier is due in late November. Hegseth fired the Joint Staff's head of plans over an Iran-war leak (28 Oct, on the record).
- **Undecided:** every report says Trump has not decided. His tone eased to "We have time, frankly" and "a guarantee there'll never be a nuclear weapon" (30 Oct). The second is softer than the US demand that Iran's uranium be "disposed".
- **The pledge:** the written promise not to strike before the midterms held, but was not restated this week. It says nothing about after 3 November.

**Tehran's mirror.** Two written statements came out in the name of Mojtaba Khamenei, still unseen on day 246:
- **27 Oct:** end "neither war nor peace" on Iran's conditions.
- **31 Oct:** "any level or type of threat must be met with an immediate, impactful and geographically unrestricted response".

**What it means.** The two tracks are one strategy: pressure with a deadline. The evidence for a deal and for a strike both strengthened in the same five days.

### December Brent expired at a six-month high, $117.64, on the war's first successful limpet-mine attack.
**The week.** Mediation headlines took about $3 off 23 October's $116.72 by 27 October ($113.88). Then, on 29 October, a small boat fixed two charges to the tanker *Louise 1* off Khasab: the war's first successful limpet attack. UKMTO, the UK navy's maritime authority, verified the method; the FT reports the attackers were Iranian. December Brent settled at **$117.64 (+2.4%)** on 30 October, its 28th straight close above $100. January, the new front month, is $113.43.

**The ship war.** The limpet was part of a burst: 4–5 hulls hit in about 40 hours, after a five-day lull. October was the **worst month of the war: "just over 30" attacks by UKMTO's count (32 per Al Jazeera)**, more than double September.
- Iran calls every hit a "mine strike" on ships using an "unauthorised southern route"; CENTCOM calls Iran's claims "FALSE".
- 35 Indian seafarers were aboard three of the ships hit, and all are safe (Shipping Minister Sonowal).
- Kpler has Hormuz crude exports at 7.3 mb/d in the week to 28 October (reported; not like-for-like with mid-October's ~14 mb/d, which included Gulf of Oman transfers). Saudi October exports held at ~6.5 mb/d by Vortexa's count (Kpler 5.4–5.5, TankerTrackers ~7.0).

**What it means.** The market is still pricing an event, not lost barrels:
- The December-2027 contract is ~$88.
- The December–January spread is in the top 1% since 1990.
- The spot–futures gap is ~$9–12.

UBS (to $110 by end-2026) and ANZ (to $105) each raised forecasts by $10. Goldman holds $90 for December but now sizes the war premium at ~$27.

### The Houthis kept firing at Riyadh after the Tehran meeting — and Ras Tanura caught fire, with Riyadh silent on why.
**The meeting.** Saudi Arabia's defence minister, Pakistan's army chief Munir and Türkiye's intelligence chief Kalin met President Pezeshkian on 26 October. They "agreed to continue talks" but produced no text and no mechanism. The National reports that Iran said it "lacked leverage" over the Houthis.

**The strikes.**
- **Riyadh:** interceptions over the city or its province came on 26, 27, 29, 30 and 31 October. The test of seven quiet days before 9 November is failing.
- **Ras Tanura, 27 October.** A fire broke out at the kingdom's main Gulf export and refining hub, and the Houthis claim a missile hit. Bloomberg's source says only that a fire near the terminal was contained; NASA fire data show heat at the terminal. One open-source analyst reads satellite imagery as showing a damaged refinery unit, but the refinery was already damaged and shut in March, so new damage is not established.
- **Riyadh's response:** Aramco and the government have said nothing, unlike earlier in October. Crude loadings continued, so any risk is to diesel and jet fuel.

**The claims.**
- The Houthis claim hits on Abqaiq and Rabigh, unconfirmed. On 30 October they denied any operation against the kingdom.
- The coalition called that day's drones over Riyadh province only "hostile". That leaves open an Iraqi second front, which is possible but not shown.
- The coalition said drones were intercepted in the Makkah and Madinah **regions**, which are provinces; it did not say the holy cities were the targets.
- AP reports Russian satellites passed over the targets of 40 Houthi and Iraqi-militia attacks. That rests on one firm's analysis.

**Unchanged.** Washington has not struck the Houthis. There has been no attributed attack on a non-Saudi merchant ship in the Red Sea in ~50 days. OPEC+ meets tomorrow and is expected to hold output.

### India had a strong week — carried by the Fed and the RBI, not by oil.
**The week.** The Nifty reclaimed 23,000 (23,053, +2.0%), with India VIX at 15.3. The rupee closed back below 95 (94.93) and the 10-year eased to 7.41%. Foreign investors bought ₹6,305 crore, their first buying week in ten, though never more than two sessions running.

**Why.** Brent rose all the same. The drivers were relief at the Fed's hold and the RBI, which reportedly defended ~95.26 and put ₹1.5 lakh crore back into the banks. That cash reversed the 26-October "drained to ₹542 crore" reading.

**The cost is building:**
- **The IMF (29 Oct):** "the rupee should be the first line of defense."
- **The RBI's forward book** (dollars it has promised to deliver later) hit a record $200bn net short at end-August. Most falls due beyond a year, which erodes usable reserves.
- **Reserves** fell a seventh week, to $719.75bn, but on gold prices: foreign-currency assets rose $1.36bn. The first intervention-week print is due on 6 November.
- **The fiscal deficit** reached 42.1% of the year's target in six months.
- **Inflation and rates:** October inflation (12 Nov) is expected above 6%. HSBC sees a 50bp hike on 4 December; ICRA sees 25bp.

**Oil companies.** The state fuel retailers lose ~₹20 a litre on diesel behind frozen pump prices (ICRA's estimate). IOC still booked a ₹21,630 crore Q2 profit on the summer price dip and LPG compensation, and warned of inventory losses if crude falls. Their shares barely moved: the market still treats the RBI's dollar window as a safety net. Reliance's oil-to-chemicals (refining and petrochemicals) EBITDA rose 68%.

**People.** MEA counts 22 Indians killed since February, including 14 seafarers; Air India has suspended Saudi flights, now including Jeddah, to 14 November.

**Monday.** India opens into Friday's US tech rout, which it did not trade.

### The AI credit scare deepened at Alphabet and spread to Amazon — and again moved US rates more than the war did.
**What happened (company filings):**
- **Alphabet** spent $9bn more on capex than its operations generated in the third quarter, its second straight negative quarter, and fell 12.6% on 29 October, its worst day ever.
- **Amazon** spent ~$77bn against ~$42bn of operating cash and borrowed $48bn in the quarter.
- **Microsoft** stayed cash-flow positive, then sold $25bn of bonds, its first since 2017.
- **Intel** fell 28.6% on 30 October, and chip stocks fell 7.2%.

**Rates and gold.**
- **Bonds:** the US 10-year closed at 4.94%, its second straight close below 5%, while oil rose 2.4% and inflation expectations did not move. Fed Chair Warsh said rates "rolled over to the downside" as oil climbed.
- **The Fed** held on 28 October by 10–2, with two votes for a hike. A December hike is ~80% priced, after 3.4% growth and 3.6% PCE inflation.
- **Gold** fell to $3,899, a one-year low, on a day real yields also fell. It failed as a hedge for the second time this month.
- **Korea:** KOSPI rose 6.6% on SK hynix's record quarter, then gave most of it back.

### European gas crossed €100 for the first time since 2022.
TTF touched €100 on 28 October, and the December contract settled ~€103.6 on 30 October.
- **Supply:** Qatar extended LNG force majeure to end-December and shipped 0.6–1.3 Mt in October, a fifth of pre-war volume at most (estimates conflict).
- **Storage:** EU storage (~75%) is the lowest for the time of year since records began in 2011.
- **Risk:** traders fear Iranian retaliation on Gulf energy.

**The ECB** held at 2.50% on 29 October after debating a hike, and conditionally signalled December. Lagarde leaves early, on 7 February.

**The winners are American.** Cheniere raised guidance (+10% on the week), and Qatar signed a second deal to *buy* US LNG.

### Israel's election settled nothing, leaving Netanyahu as caretaker through the strike window.
**The result.** The final count gives Netanyahu's bloc 53 seats, Eisenkot's opposition 48 and Arab parties a record 19. Fitch expects another election in April.

**Iran.** Netanyahu's election-night theme was a "once-in-a-lifetime opportunity" with Trump to "remove this threat once and for all". He is reported to be lobbying for a joint operation right after 3 November. There was no Israel–Iran fire in the window.

### The quiet threads held.
- **No US strike on Iranian territory** has been confirmed since 1 September. A "self-defence strike" on Qeshm (29–30 Oct) rests on an anonymous official and a deleted post. CENTCOM's spokesman called the claim that it had acknowledged strikes "false".
- **No Iranian missile salvo** has been fired at a state in ~51 days.
- **The nuclear sites were untouched** for a 13th window. The IAEA sees no rebuilding but cannot verify ~440 kg of enriched uranium.
- **Lebanon:** Hezbollah has not fired. Israeli strikes in Lebanon continue.
- **Pezeshkian–Putin (27 Oct):** nothing on uranium custody.
- **US opinion:** Americans oppose the war 57–36, but only 6% call it their top issue.

### The Signal You Might Miss
The calm in physical oil depends on a fleet that just became a target.

Gulf barrels reach buyers through a shuttle: tankers load inside the Gulf, run the strait dark and transfer their cargo off Oman. About a fifth of the world's supertankers are tied up in it (Braemar), with ~490 ships waiting at anchor outside the strait. That shuttle is why spot prices came back toward futures, and why the graph reads $117 as an event premium, not a shortage.

A limpet mine needs no launcher, leaves no radar track and can be denied. It suits exactly those anchored ships.

In the graph, the new `strait-of-hormuz` anchored-limpet watch sits on edges already at 10.0 (`→ shipping-tankers`, `→ brent-crude`). It sits beside a physical gap that re-widened to $9–12 after briefly dipping below $10. If limpets reach the anchorage, that gap widens again without any US strike: 4 November is not the only route to $130.
