## I. What Happened

### At sea, at least two tankers were hit on Tuesday, one inside UAE waters, and India says one of its sailors was hurt. Over Iran, a second quiet night, and Rubio said on the record that "for the time being… no need for major combat operations". Oil held $90 at the settle and is up 1% this morning.
**At sea.**
- **UAE waters (confirmed by the owner):** ADNOC's shipping arm said one of its vessels "sustained damage following an attack in the UAE's territorial waters, in the Arabian Gulf" on Tuesday afternoon (WAM, the UAE state agency). It named no ship and no attacker.
- **India's shipping minister** said the supertanker *Fujairah Energy*, reportedly sailing from Ruwais to Dubai, was hit by a projectile. Four crew were injured; one Indian had minor injuries. A ship registry lists ADNOC's shipping arm as its manager, so it is probably the same ship. Neither has said so.
- **Off Oman (reported):** UKMTO, the UK navy's shipping-advice centre, logged a tanker hit by an "unidentified projectile" about 19 nautical miles north-east of Lima, at the strait's southern exit, at 19:08 IST. The crew is safe. Early on Wednesday it logged "suspicious activity" involving a tanker in the strait.
- **Claimed:** the Revolutionary Guards say a third, unnamed tanker "exploded on striking a naval mine" (our translation) in the eastern strait.
- **The count (via Reuters):** the Washington Institute counts 37 ships attacked in October, the most of any month of the war, and 10 so far in November.

**Over Iran.** No strike was reported on night eight, the second quiet night running.
- **Rubio, on the record in Paris:** the president has "concluded that, for the time being, there's no need for major combat operations because we're achieving the same objective through economic means" (the transcript as relayed). Iran's conditions are "absurd".
- **Hegseth, at the Pentagon:** "We control the Strait of Hormuz"; "if that regime makes one wrong move… we remain locked and loaded." He claimed no November strike.

**The price (confirmed).**
- **Tuesday's settle:** January Brent settled at **$91.49**, down 1.17%. That is the lowest close since 31 August (CNBC; Reuters). It had touched $89.15 after the US inflation figures. Eurasia Group's Gregory Brew had said Brent was unlikely to fall below $90 without "more durable signs of de-escalation and a US-Iran deal"; on the settle, that held.
- **The rebound:** in nine minutes from 21:45 IST on Tuesday, Brent jumped from $90.86 to $92.07. It was oil-specific: US share futures were flat, while gold fell and the dollar firmed after the Dallas Fed's Logan spoke two minutes earlier (21:43 IST).
- **Wednesday:** **$92.43** at 11:40 IST, up about 1%.

**What moved it.** The links to the price below were made by the outlets named, not by us.
- **The rebound:** Reuters ties it to Iran's security chief, Mohsen Rezaei, telling the Lebanese channel Al-Mayadeen that there were no talks.
  - **The timing does not settle it.** The first publication of the interview we found (Tasnim, 21:52 IST) came seven minutes after the jump began, and we could not find when Al-Mayadeen aired it.
  - **Two other items came shortly before:** Netanyahu's call to topple Iran's government (~21:31 IST) and the UKMTO report off Oman (first relay we found, 21:29 IST). No outlet tied either to the price.
- **Wednesday:** Reuters: "Hormuz shipping attacks offset Iran truce hopes."

### Tehran called talk of talks "a pure lie" and tied Hormuz to its seven conditions.
- **Rezaei, to Al-Mayadeen:**
  - "The Americans' claim about continuing negotiations or holding a meeting in Qatar is a pure lie."
  - "At the present moment, we are not conducting any negotiations with the Americans" (Mehr's translation). The US "initial response was insufficient and deceptive".
  - "Until these conditions are fully implemented, the Strait of Hormuz will not be reopened."
  - Iranian state outlets added, in their own words, that US talk of talks is meant to push oil prices down. ("Insufficient" repeats his 11-October line.)
- **Qatar** has said nothing new since Tuesday's "positive responses from both sides". The claimed Vance–Rezaei meeting in Doha now faces two on-record Iranian denials.
- **The Leader, Mojtaba Khamenei:** nothing since 4 November (day 257).
- **A date our last brief missed:** on Sunday Trump said he thinks he will meet Putin and Xi at the Asia-Pacific summit in Shenzhen on 18–19 November: "We're trying to end the war in Iran." China's foreign ministry is "in communication with relevant parties regarding a meeting between the three heads of state". No capital has confirmed the meeting.
- **Threats in the West:** Treasury Secretary Bessent was moved onto a military base over Iranian threats (the New York Times; a senior official to Fox). The US charged three men over a September plot at RAF Fairford in Britain; Iran denies involvement.

### Egypt says its forces are already in Saudi Arabia for defence and that it is reinforcing them. The Houthis disowned the pipeline hit Saudi Arabia confirmed.
- **Egypt (confirmed by Cairo):** Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty told France 24 (aired Monday; carried by AFP on Tuesday) that Egypt "already" has "a military presence there at the request of" Saudi Arabia. It began "a few months ago", "for defensive purposes", and Egypt is strengthening it.
  - **The air-defence role:** the reports that carry "air defence capabilities to defend the various sensitive sites in Saudi Arabia, including of course the holy sites" tie it to the reinforcement, not clearly to the forces already there. France 24's own page and Xinhua do not carry the phrase.
  - Riyadh has not commented, and no weapon system has been named on the record.
- **The pipeline:**
  - **The denial:** the Houthis called the Saudi account of a hit in the Madinah region "a lie and a grave slander". Riyadh had never named them.
  - **Their own claims (unconfirmed):** they say they hit the line's pumping stations "in Riyadh", King Khalid airport and the "financial center" in Riyadh, and Aramco at Abqaiq. They also warned "all investors and companies" against keeping money in Saudi Arabia.
  - **Where that leaves it:** the one hit Saudi Arabia has confirmed has no claimant. When the line was hit in September, Riyadh said the drones came from Iraq.
- **The Saudi cabinet** called on Tuesday for "the immediate halt to all acts that threaten maritime navigation" in the Red Sea, Bab el-Mandeb and Hormuz. It did not name the Houthis, as its three October meetings had.
- **Overnight:** no new launch or Riyadh alert found (early in the day there).
- **Markets:** Aramco fell 1.9% on Tuesday, its biggest one-day fall in six months; two Arab outlets tie it to oil. IndiGo suspended its Saudi flights until 21–30 November.

  - **Our rough arithmetic:** energy added at most ~0.03 points of the 0.1-point surprise. Core prices and food did the rest.
- **Shares and gold:** the S&P 500 fell **0.38%**. Nvidia fell **5.47%** on reports about its chief executive and an alleged Chinese spy (the FBI is investigating, Trump said). CoreWeave beat and raised its forecasts after the close, and Asian chip stocks rallied on Wednesday (Taiwan's index closed up ~3.6%). Gold fell **2.05%** to $4,030.
