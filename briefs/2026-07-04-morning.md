## I. What Happened

### Iran hit back within 24 hours — then both sides stood down, and the deal survived its first real test.
The binary the 27 June brief left open is resolved, in the least obvious way: **Iran counter-struck, and the deal still held.** Early on 28 June the IRGC fired ballistic missiles and drones at US bases in Kuwait (Ali Al Salem, Ahmad Al Jaber) and Bahrain (Sheikh Isa and the Fifth Fleet HQ at Port Salman); Qatar's defence ministry separately reported Al Udeid was hit, with one Qatari civilian killed (that element rests on a single official chain). The retaliation was calibrated to be survivable — most munitions intercepted, one residential building damaged in Bahrain, **zero American casualties** — exactly mirroring Washington's own "enforcement" logic (sources: IRGC statement via Press TV; Al Jazeera; AP/PBS; US official via Reuters). It followed a wider exchange than first reported: after the initial US strike night, an Iranian drone hit the tanker *Kiku* in Hormuz on 27 June, and the US ran a **second strike night** against Iranian surveillance, communications and minelayer targets (CENTCOM). Then, within 24 hours, **both sides agreed to stand down** — "vessels can move freely," per a senior US official — and the halt has held for six days with no confirmed incident. Neither side repudiated the MoU; indirect talks convened in Doha on 30 June and produced three concrete things: a standing US-Iran communication channel, a goods-purchase mechanism against the first $6bn of frozen Iranian funds, and an agreed focus on restoring Hormuz passage. Qatar called it "positive progress"; Iran insists the talks were indirect, and the nuclear file was not touched — whatever Trump's "de-nuking of Iran is moving along well" suggests.

### The war premium never re-armed: oil shrugged off a US-Iran missile exchange and fell for a fourth straight week.
The 27 June brief expected oil to re-price the strike at Monday's open. The market's answer: **+0.9% for one session, then four straight down days.** Brent settled $71.57 on Wednesday and ended the holiday-shortened week around $72 — at or below the pre-war baseline — capping a fourth consecutive weekly loss and a June of **−21%, the worst month since March 2020** (CNBC settlements; The National). The mechanism is supply, not sentiment: Hormuz transit counts roughly tripled week-on-week (43 on 1 July vs ~12 the prior week — though flow-quality data conflicts; see Section III), delayed Gulf liftings are moving, Iranian post-blockade exports surged, and OPEC+ is expected to add another ~188k bpd for August at Sunday's meeting. Citi now calls **$60 Brent by year-end** on surplus fundamentals; Goldman holds the opposite skew (Q4 cut to $80, but risk to the upside on 7.75-year-low US inventories and "the possibility Hormuz never fully reopens"). The graph's **Brent-below-$85 trigger formally fired** this week — 12+ consecutive sessions — and with it, India's importer-dividend trigger. Note the asymmetry has flipped since 27 June: the remaining downside is fundamentals and won't reverse on a final deal; the upside on re-escalation, against no inventory cushion, is violent.

### Everything now freezes for Khamenei's funeral — with the deal's two hardest files conspicuously unresolved.
Iran opened the delayed state funeral of Ali Khamenei on 3 July — Tehran processions through 6 July, Qom on the 7th, Najaf/Karbala on the 8th, burial at Mashhad's Imam Reza shrine on the 9th — and the Doha talks are paused until it ends; officials claim 15–20m mourners (regime figures). Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei **will not attend his father's funeral**: NBC reports he remains severely injured from the 28 February strike and runs the state by courier, unseen in public for four months. The pause lands on a hardening domestic stage: RFE/RL and Iranian media report that 63 of the Assembly of Experts' 88 members publicly urged negotiators to hold the supreme leader's "red lines," with the same bloc reportedly declaring Trump and Netanyahu "Mahdoor al-Dam" (blood may lawfully be shed) — and Pezeshkian rushed to Qom to enlist senior clerics **against his own spoilers** (IranWire; RFE/RL). Meanwhile the deal's verification pillar went backwards: parliament speaker Ghalibaf called reports of expanded IAEA access "lies" — **no inspectors have entered Fordow, Natanz, Isfahan or any bombed site**, leaving 440.9 kg of 60% HEU unverified — and Iran's transit-toll demand for Hormuz is now a formal negotiation file via an Omani proposal the US is lobbying against (Axios). Roughly 44 days remain on the 60-day roadmap, six of them consumed by mourning. The week to watch begins ~10 July: talks either resume, or the funeral becomes the pretext for drift.

### Lebanon is now the likeliest kill-vector — and Tehran has explicitly wired it into the talks.
While the US-Iran seam held, the Israel-Hezbollah one frayed on schedule. Israel struck southern Lebanon on 28 June, two days after the Rubio framework, even as Netanyahu hailed that framework as "historic" — the negotiate-while-bombing pattern the graph treats as one strategy, not a contradiction. Hezbollah, which was not a party to a deal that mandates its disarmament, has declared it "null and void." The week's sharpest exchange: a Hezbollah gunman seriously wounded an Israeli reservist on 2 July (IDF-confirmed; notably unclaimed by Hezbollah), and Israel answered on the 3rd by hitting **ten Hezbollah infrastructure sites** plus a weapons truck (AP wire — largely a single-wire account). The escalation that matters is verbal but explicit: Ghalibaf tied Israel's Lebanon strikes directly to Iran's willingness to keep negotiating — "the Islamic Republic will resume its proportionate measures" if commitments aren't honored. That is rhetoric, not action — but it converts every 48–72-hour militia cycle in south Lebanon into a potential detonator under the Doha track.

### India got its importer dividend back — while foreign investors closed a record half-year exodus.
Indian markets spent Monday and Tuesday hostage to the strike exchange, then decoupled for three straight up sessions once Doha convened: the Nifty ended the week **+0.9% at 24,270.85**, India VIX at a one-month low, and the 10-year G-sec at **6.72% — a fifteen-week low** — with FPIs buying Indian debt even as they sold equities. Oil marketing companies led the reassertion of the dividend (HPCL +3.6%, BPCL +3.3%, IOC +2.75% on 2 July; PSU retailers pointedly did NOT match private Nayara's ₹5/₹3 pump cuts, rebuilding margins instead). The counterweights are real: FIIs sold equities every confirmed day of the week (June closed at **−₹49,340 cr; H1 2026 is a record −₹2.74 lakh cr / $29.3bn exodus**, absorbed by DIIs), and the rupee still ended weaker at 95.21 despite the RBI spending **$5.65bn of reserves** in the intervention week — an unintervened rupee was headed for 95.50+. Domestic tailwinds (record ₹1.94 lakh cr June GST, +13.9%) now face two non-crisis tests: the IMD's below-normal July monsoon forecast, and the 24 July expiry of the temporary 10% US tariff regime — a correction, incidentally: the operative trade deadline is July 24, not July 9.

### The crisis premium has migrated from oil to logistics — tankers, insurance and boxes are the last markets still pricing the war.
While crude sits at pre-war levels, VLCC fixtures for Hormuz transits are printing near **$470,000/day** (Lloyd's List/Seatrade), Baltic TD3C rates — normalizing from $412k mid-June to roughly $218–313k depending on the print — remain 2–4x the historical norm, and Frontline jumped +5.9% Thursday (+57% YTD). Marine war-risk insurance **did not soften on the stand-down**: Marsh-sourced premiums stand at 3–8% of hull value (vs 0.25% pre-crisis), with underwriters demanding 2–4 weeks of confirmed calm and six P&I clubs still withholding cover. Container freight is the one shipping market still *building* premium — Drewry's WCI rose 9% w/w to $4,530/FEU on the 24-July US tariff-cliff rush plus Gulf war-risk surcharges. Adjacent cascades: LME aluminium fell 4.4% Thursday to cap the **steepest monthly drop since 2008** as bottled-up Gulf smelter exports (~10% of global output) return through Hormuz — a positioning unwind into a physically tight market — and the helium squeeze reached price tags, with Nippon Sanso raising all Japanese helium prices >30% from July and Qatar's Helium2 restart set back by the 21–22 June Ras Laffan explosion. One non-crisis mover to keep separate: Thursday's semiconductor selloff (SMH −4.5%, Micron −10%) was an Apple-China-DRAM and antitrust story, not a war story.

### Marsh / Lloyd's List — the insurance-freight contradiction (market voice)
Underwriters held Gulf war-risk at 3–8% of hull value after the 27–28 June exchange and want **2–4 weeks of confirmed de-escalation** before repricing; Frontline meanwhile forecasts "violent" rate moves in both directions. **Read:** freight is paying urgency, insurance is pricing recurrence — both are pricing the same residual risk, and both collapse together only on a durable deal. That collapse (VLCC rates toward $80–100k/day) is the single most reversible crisis trade on the board.

### Source Tone Assessment
The split inside Iran's media IS the story: IRGC organs escalatory (Fars: US forces "will experience hell"; a floated-then-disavowed nuclear-deterrence commentary) while IRNA and the foreign ministry run transactional and implementation-first — and Press TV now anchors defiance *inside* the deal, casting US strikes as "a breach of Clause One." Both governments are framing force as deal-enforcement. US messaging is de-escalatory beneath the bluster (Fox led with the off-ramp; Trump went from "Iran will no longer exist" to "they've come a long way" in five days). Gulf state media is hardening — The National: the MoU's "14 points don't address Gulf security... Iran's drones and missiles remain a clear and present danger" — while still backing mediation. Indian business media demoted West Asia from front-page war to a pre-market cue within three sessions. European framing is the coldest: the calm is an inventory-runway story (IEA released 400m barrels in March; US SPR near 1983 lows), and the runway is finite.

Set that against what the logistics complex is saying (war-risk insurance at 12–30x pre-crisis, $470k/day Hormuz fixtures, six P&I clubs withholding cover) and against the graph's three live re-gap vectors — the Lebanon militia cycle Ghalibaf just wired into the talks, the post-funeral resumption test (~10 July), and any single Hormuz enforcement incident — and equity volatility is the cheapest crisis convexity of the cycle. ### Risk Landscape

**Deployment (India):** the 27 June defensive/hold posture is superseded — the binary resolved benignly and the importer dividend is banked again. But the FII condition is still unmet: foreigners sold every confirmed day (a conflicting Friday print — possibly the first net-buy day — needs Monday's NSE archive). **Conditions to add risk:** (1) talks visibly resume post-funeral (~10–14 July) without a new kinetic exchange; (2) FII flows print net-positive for 3+ sessions; (3) Lebanon stays contained through the funeral window. Two of three would justify moving from watchful to incremental deployment into the domestic-demand complex; none justifies chasing the IT rally, which is a US-rates trade, not an India story. The next 72 hours are cheap information: OPEC+ Sunday, first full session Monday, funeral through Thursday.
