## I. What Happened

### The Fed hiked for the first time in three years — unanimously — and the market's read on this war was confirmed in a single session: gold fell, the rupee broke 96, the 10-year crossed 5%.
The FOMC raised the funds rate 25bp to 3.75–4.00% on Wednesday, 12–0 (the three July dissenters who wanted a hike became the whole committee), with a dot plot whose median calls for one more increase this year. The statement named supply shocks in "energy"; Chair Warsh said "the plain fact is that inflation is too high and has been for too long" and, on oil, "we cannot affect any individual price" — the Fed is hiking on a number it disclaims. The reaction was the graph's regime test resolving in one afternoon: the US 10-year closed at 5.02%, its first close above 5% since the war began; the dollar index pushed above 100; **gold fell ~1.1% on the decision and trades ~$4,330 this morning** — through a week with a proxy holding a second chokepoint and a drone intercepted over Mecca — confirming that the `fed → gold` edge now carries a negative sign and that this war is priced as inflation, not systemic risk; the Dow lost 1.2% and financials 1.5%. President Trump wrote that rates "should be 1%, or less… LOWER THE INTEREST RATES… AND FAST!" but said he keeps confidence in Warsh, so the independence fight the market feared did not arrive. The CBO put the causal chain on the record the day before: the war has added ~0.5pp to PCE inflation by early 2027 and more than 40% of second-quarter PCE inflation was Hormuz/Red Sea energy. The next binary is 28 October — a second hike unless Brent is visibly falling by then.

### Brent settled above $100 for a sixth straight session, printed a four-month closing high of $108.75, then fell 2.7% on a restart timeline for Saudi Arabia's pipeline that no tracker has yet confirmed.
Tuesday's spike had five named drivers (Reuters): Yanbu loadings suspended, Aramco cancelling late-September European cargoes, Houthi fire on Saudi targets, the Gulf–Iran meeting's postponement and a Libyan field shutdown. Wednesday's reversal to $105.83 had two: **Bloomberg's company sources said Aramco is targeting half the East-West line's capacity "within days"** by bypassing the damaged section (full capacity in ~six weeks), and US Energy Secretary Chris Wright told CNBC the outage "will be measured in days" — plus a surprise 7mb API crude build and EIA product builds that crushed gasoline (−6.8%) and heating oil (−5.0%) from Tuesday's record. **Nothing has restarted**: Vortexa shows no Saudi crude has left Yanbu since 11 September, AP puts pumping-station repairs at three to five weeks, and independent analysts reading satellite imagery say weeks; the graph's `saudi-arabia` RESTART trigger — an Aramco statement or tracker-verified Yanbu loadings above 2 mb/d — did not fire, and the "days" claim now has a clock on it that expires around 23 September. The screen also hides the physical market: **Dated Brent traded above $130 (Bloomberg, Energy Intelligence — first time since April; Reuters put it nearer $122), a physical premium of $15–20+ over futures and the widest paper-physical gap of the cycle**; Shanghai crude reportedly hit a record $138; US retail diesel a record $6.27. The five-session test the 14 September brief set to falsify Goldman's "severe restriction" scenario has fired in Goldman's favour; Goldman now says the Saudi attacks "increase the probability" of Brent above $120.

### Washington is talking the war down on a midterm calendar — and for the first time the constraints behind that are official documents rather than leaks.
Vice-President Vance told the *New York Post* that "there are really two phases to this thing, and the first phase is done" — the nuclear programme, conventional military and power projection destroyed — and that the conflict "will enter a much different phase in a couple of months"; Trump said Iran "wants to make a deal, quickly and badly" and on Thursday morning "hopefully we are towards the end of the war"; Axios reports he will meet all six GCC states at the UN on Tuesday 22 September to discuss a "postwar strategy" whose "day-after plan" is to be finalised after the 3 November election. All of this is rhetoric and scheduling — the only US kinetic act this window was Monday's drone strike on two IRGC boats that CENTCOM says tried to seize a US unmanned vessel (Iran says fishermen died). What is not rhetoric: **the House passed its third war-powers resolution 220–204 on Tuesday**, with seven Republicans including two from battleground Iowa who flipped ("with the negotiating window closed, sustained combat operations now require congressional authorization" — Rep. Zach Nunn), and Speaker Johnson then sent the House home until mid-November, killing both a Hegseth impeachment vote and any further Iran legislation before the election. The fuel for those votes was two official accountings: **the Pentagon Inspector General put the war at $33.4bn through June with "strategic inventory shortfalls" and the first acknowledged F-35 hit by Iranian fire; the CBO put it at $38.1bn through 1 August and said missile-defence interceptors are one-half to two-thirds depleted, with a five-year rebuild.** Tehran's security council answered the deal talk within hours: "No talks until Iran's conditions are met. Period!"

### Tehran spoke with two voices in one day — and its state media chose the harder one.
President Pezeshkian, interviewed by *India Today* during the BRICS summit, said Iran is "ready to hold talks with the United States, including on its nuclear program," inside the NPT, if Washington lifts sanctions and stops "negotiating through force" — ruling out an immediate return to direct talks because trust is "badly damaged." Within hours Mohsen Rezaei, the former IRGC commander who now runs the Supreme National Security Council, said on Press TV: "Don't get distracted by the U.S. president's mixed signals… No talks until Iran's conditions are met. Period!" — an end to the war and blockade, frozen assets released, a region-wide ceasefire including Lebanon and Gaza — and Press TV did not mention the president. The same day, per a single aggregated report, a hardline MP moved to have Pezeshkian declared politically incompetent and the Speaker refused, one member calling it a "coup plot." Abroad, Foreign Minister Araghchi told Beijing that Iran "has already agreed with Oman on a plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz"; Tasnim had already said that plan reopens nothing until US conditions are met, and Iran still wants transit fees Oman rejects. Mojtaba Khamenei is 202 days unseen, and Tehran has still not answered the *New York Times* account that July's ship attacks were unauthorised. Five missiles struck Iranian-Kurdish opposition camps in Iraqi Kurdistan on Monday (no casualties — Rudaw, Kurdish security agency), an attack the Kurdish authorities attribute to Tehran, which has not commented; and the Mahsa Amini anniversary general strike shuttered the Kurdish cities on Wednesday despite security pressure. The graph's reading is unchanged and now on the record: no Iranian institution can currently deliver the deal Washington says Iran wants.

### India's CPI came in below the trigger and the market ignored it: the rupee opened at ₹96, gilts hit a one-year high, and the sell-side moved as one to an October RBI hike.
August CPI printed **4.82%** on Monday evening — below the 4.9% poll, the 4.86% Bloomberg median and the graph's 5.0% trigger (rural 5.23%, urban 4.31%, food 5.95%) — but the same week WPI printed 8.3% on fuel and power, and HSBC, Nomura and Standard Chartered now all forecast 25bp hikes on 7 October *and* in December (Nomura sees headline CPI through 6% in Oct–Nov). The Nifty made a five-month low of 23,118 on Tuesday — within ~120 points of the "tests 23,000" level the 14 September brief warned of, on exactly the mechanism it specified (Brent $107 and US yields above 5%, not CPI; financials −1.8%, autos −2%) — then recovered to ~23,275 on domestic buying (DIIs +₹3,908 crore Wednesday). **The rupee opened Thursday at ₹96.00, its first 96-handle since late July**, within hours of the Fed's hike; Reuters cites three traders saying the RBI likely intervened; the record 96.60 is 0.6% away. The 10-year gilt is at 7.06%, up 10bp in three sessions. One gap the reader should know about: NSDL has not updated foreign-flow data beyond the ₹13,138 crore outflow to 11 September already reported, so the ₹25,000 crore September trigger cannot be assessed. One Indian seafarer from the tanker *El Gaia* is still missing off Oman.

### The Houthis have held the Bab el-Mandeb for six days without touching a merchant ship — while a drone intercepted south of Mecca has cost them Qatar's neutrality, at least rhetorically.
The trigger the 14 September brief said would convert the Houthi position into a general chokepoint — a verified attack on a non-Saudi hull — has not fired: UKMTO, Ambrey and the Joint Maritime Information Center log no merchant attack in the southern Red Sea since Perim fell. What did happen: at 18:50 on Tuesday Saudi air defences intercepted a drone "south of Makkah," the first alert over Mecca in nearly a decade; the coalition attributes it to the Houthis and calls the holy mosques a "red line"; **the Houthis categorically deny it** ("our operations target their oil facilities and military bases, far removed from holy places") and claim, without evidence, to have downed a Saudi F-15 over Marib. Qatar — the Gulf's mediator and Al Jazeera's owner — called the incident "a shameful act of aggression… a grave escalation" and separately warned that closing the Bab el-Mandeb would be "catastrophic for the entire world." The Gulf's military geometry also moved: Axios reports, with CENTCOM confirmation, that the chiefs of the US, Israel and seven Arab states (Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Jordan, Egypt) met secretly in Germany — the first joint Israel–Gulf table of the war, where Adm. Cooper said US forces "will not withdraw" and briefed plans to expand Hormuz traffic — and Riyadh, holding fire on Iraq for a fifth day, sent MBS to receive Cooper in Jeddah and then to Cairo. On Hormuz itself a US official told Axios 40 ships a day now transit under American protection with daytime escorts; Reuters/Kpler data counted four commodity vessels on Tuesday and a ten-day average of 18 — the US counts escorted movements, but the gap is the widest yet and the graph's "lane is working" test (15+/day for five days) is not met on independent data.

### The nuclear firebreak held a ninth window with no new threat; Lebanon talked in Washington; Israel's election is still tied.
No US strike touched Fordow, Natanz, Esfahan or Pickaxe Mountain, and — for the first time since July — no new presidential threat to do so was issued; Vance's framing declares the nuclear objective achieved rather than climbing that rung. There has been no US strike on Iranian territory for nine operational periods and no Iranian ballistic launch at a host state for seven days, the longest pause since 28 August — the top of the ladder is quiet while the water is not. Lebanese and Israeli ambassadors met for ninety minutes at the State Department on Tuesday on the pilot-village model; Lebanese sources say no decisions and that Israel "refuses to withdraw"; no Hezbollah fire was recorded. Israel's 27 October race remains a dead heat — Yashar leads in four of five polls, Likud in one; the newest TV poll has Netanyahu's bloc "slightly ahead" but "well short of 61."

### The Signal You Might Miss
The obvious read of Wednesday is "oil fell because the Saudi pipeline is coming back." The graph's read is that **nothing came back — the barrels moved sideways, and two markets that should agree do not.** Tankers rose 4% on a day crude fell 2.7% because Saudi crude that cannot load at Yanbu is being escorted through Hormuz instead (the new `saudi-arabia → shipping-tankers` edge, seeded at 5.0), which is bullish tonne-miles and bearish the oil *premium* at the same time; and while the futures screen dropped $3, Dated Brent — the price a refiner actually pays for a prompt cargo — is $122–130+ depending on the assessor, a $15–20+ premium that is the widest of the cycle. That is the `brent-crude` paper-physical trigger, active since spring, now inverted and stretched: the paper market is trading the administration's calendar (Vance, the GCC meeting, "days"), the physical market is trading the strait. The same split shows up in volatility: the VIX is 17.7 after a first Fed hike in three years, a 5% ten-year, a Mecca alert and a House vote to end the war — the third time this cycle the graph has flagged equity-vol complacency against the physical complex, and the first with a hike inside it. And one de-escalation nobody is reporting: the fertilizer cascade to India — a March–April terror — has quietly died; India is covered, cheaply, from China.
