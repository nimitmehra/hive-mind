## I. What Happened

### Oil fell 7.6% on talk of a deal — and that night, US officials say, American jets struck Iran, and not for the first time.
**The talk.** On Monday, the last trading day before the vote, both capitals sounded like they wanted a deal.
- **Tehran:** President Pezeshkian said Iran's leader had given the government "certain powers" to negotiate an end to the war. This is his account; the leader's office has said nothing.
- **Trump:** "Iran wants a settlement, as soon as possible. It could happen!" To Axios: "there is no rush". Asked after Monday's close whether he is considering strikes after the midterms, he said "No".
- **Vance:** "we've certainly made some progress."

**The price.** January Brent fell from $113.43 to **$104.77**, its biggest one-day drop since July.
- **The shape:** the front of the curve fell twice as hard as the back (December 2027 −3.6%). The March contract settled at $99.04, so every contract from March on is now below $100; on Friday that line sat at June.
- **What it means:** the rally built on "strikes after the midterms" came out a day early. It was premium, not supply.

**The strike.** That night, according to US officials, a US jet destroyed an Iranian surface-to-air launcher on Qeshm Island after it fired at a US aircraft.
- **Sourcing:** at least seven US outlets have it from their own unnamed officials, and Iranian state media reported five explosions. No US official has confirmed it on the record, and Iran has not said who struck or retaliated.
- **Not the first:** the WSJ reports a "series of limited strikes" carried out quietly "for several weeks". NBC's officials say the Revolutionary Guards "routinely" fire at US aircraft over the strait.
- **Washington's framing:** self-defence, "not a resumption of major combat". The same evening Trump said "we haven't been doing any fighting for months", and that Iran's deepest nuclear site, Pickaxe Mountain, could be hit "in one hour".
- **The market's reading:** Brent recovered only ~$1.8 in Asia on Tuesday.

**What it means.** If the reporting holds, the pause in US strikes on Iran since September was quieter than it was clean: Washington runs a low-level shooting war around the strait without announcing it, and markets have learned to ignore it. So the question from 4 November is not whether the US fires, but whether it **declares** a campaign. The graph's trigger has been redefined accordingly (Section III).

### The ship war reached an Indian-flagged boat: one Indian killed off Oman, as Iran doubled its blacklist of ships.
**The attacks.** The UK navy's maritime authority (UKMTO) verified three attacks in nine hours on 1 November:
- a UAE tug left "partially sinking";
- a ship holed off Limah, on Oman's Musandam peninsula;
- a product tanker disabled, reportedly by drones, after leaving Kuwait: the farthest north this autumn surge has reached.

**The death.** On 2 November an "unidentified aerial object" hit the India-flagged trading dhow *Al Jusufi* near Limah. One of its 14 Indian crew was killed; Oman's coast guard rescued the rest.
- **New Delhi (3 Nov):** India "condemn[s]" the attack and calls for "immediate de-escalation" and "the conclusion of ongoing negotiations for a diplomatic solution". It still names no attacker.
- **The verified record:** no one has attributed any of these hits. Iran claims "mine strikes" on "unauthorised routes" that match none of them.

**The paper blockade.** Iran's strait authority added 114 ships to its blacklist (~240 in all), now including state fleets: 10 Kuwaiti, 7 Saudi (Bahri) and 6 Emirati (ADNOC) vessels (reported).
- **Al Mayeda:** Bloomberg reports that Qatar's LNG carrier, hit on 30 October, had received Iran's safe-passage approval hours earlier. Iranian permission does not guarantee passage. Qatar, the main mediator, "reserves its right" to respond.

**What did not happen.** No second limpet attack, and no hit on the ~490 ships waiting at anchor outside the strait. Two hits off Limah do put the approach to those anchorages in play.

### Physical crude now looks plentiful. Diesel does not.
**Plentiful.** Bloomberg's tanker tracking puts October exports from Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Iraq and Kuwait at 15.53 million barrels a day, up ~10% and "nearly back to pre-conflict" (reported, one source). Dubai, the Middle East's own benchmark, has slipped into contango for the first time since July: near-term barrels are cheaper than later ones, the opposite of a shortage.

**Not plentiful.** The diesel margin is still ~$81 a barrel, and US pump diesel is $6.29 a gallon.

**The rest of the week.**
- **OPEC+** held December output (1 November), as expected.
- **Aramco** lifted third-quarter adjusted profit 9% and is studying "alternative export routes". Its December prices for Asia are not out.
- **Goldman** raised its December Brent forecast from $90 to $105 the day before the fall.

### India rallied to its highest close since 23 September — but cheaper oil is not reaching the rupee.
**Equities and bonds.** The Nifty rose 1.7% to 23,440.80 on Monday and was ~23,725 at midday Tuesday.
- **Reliance:** about half of Monday's gain came from Reliance (+11.6%) after a record refining quarter.
- **IT:** Indian IT fell only 0.5% into the US tech rout.
- **Bonds:** the 10-year had its best day in six months (~7.32%, −9bp). The market is shifting December's expected RBI hike from 50bp toward 25bp.
- **Foreign investors:** they bought for a second straight session, after selling ₹34,843 crore in October.

**The rupee.** It closed at 94.84 on Monday, slightly weaker than Friday's corrected 94.76, and touched only 94.60 on Tuesday morning.
- **Why it barely moved:** dealers say the RBI and the State Bank are buying dollars into any strength, for oil payments and to rebuild a record **$239bn** of forward dollar sales. Part of that book is the summer's deposit-swap window, not all defence.
- **What it means:** oil spikes reach the currency in full, and oil falls barely do.

**India VIX** rose with the index (15.3 → ~16): option buyers are hedging 4 November.

### The AI scare became a funding scare, and Asia's chip markets fell 3.6%.
**The raises (filings and reports):**

- Anthropic's filing shows ~$895bn of commitments.

**The damage.**

- **India diverged on price:** the Nifty rose on Tuesday while Korea and Taiwan fell. Foreigners sold ₩1.52trn of Korean stocks on Tuesday; in India they bought on 30 October and 2 November, and Tuesday's figure is due ~18:00 IST. That is the pattern our graph has waited for, but the flows are not yet shown on the same day.

**US rates.** The 10-year closed at 4.91%, its third straight close below 5%. For the first time this run the driver was oil (inflation expectations −5bp), not AI. Corporate credit spreads widened through 30 October, and bond-ETF prices point wider again on Monday.

### The Houthi test failed — but Riyadh's attackers are increasingly unnamed.
**The test.** The Saudi-led coalition downed two drones "in the Riyadh region" on 2 November, so the test of seven quiet days over Riyadh before 9 November cannot now pass.
- **The caveat:** three of the last four interceptions over the Riyadh area had no named launcher, and the Houthis claimed none of them.
- **What is attributed or claimed:** the coalition attributes daily launches at the south to the Houthis, and the Houthis claimed strikes on Dammam airport and a Jazan radar that Riyadh has not confirmed. A Dammam fire was at air-conditioning depots, per civil defence, which gave no cause.
- **Their threat:** investing in Aramco is "a venture fraught with losses".

**Saudi response.** Saudi jets struck the Houthi port of Ras Isa.

**Unchanged.** No attack on a non-Saudi ship in the Red Sea for ~53 days, and no US strike on Yemen.

### Israel has no government in sight, and its caretaker reportedly disagrees with Trump on striking now.
**The count.** Eisenkot, who leads the opposition bloc, has 48 firm recommendations (about 67 if both Arab lists join). Netanyahu has roughly 46–53. No one has a 61-seat path.
- **Certification:** Israel's election chief, security service chief and attorney-general jointly called the vote "conducted properly". Haaretz reports Likud is weighing refusing to recognise the result.
- **Timetable:** results are certified on 4 November; President Herzog consults 4–5 November and must choose a candidate by 11 November.

**Iran.** Axios and AP report that Netanyahu pressed Trump to strike right after the vote. Israel's UN envoy denies it, and Channel 12 says Trump wants Israel to stay out of the diplomacy.

**Lebanon.** Through US mediators, Israel has reportedly told Beirut that fighting in Lebanon resumes if Hezbollah joins an Iran war. Hezbollah has not fired.

### European gas fell back below €100 as storage began to fall.
December TTF fell ~9% to about €95 on the same headlines.
- **Storage:** EU storage has started to fall (74.6%); the IEA calls it the lowest entering winter since 2013.
- **Supply:** Qatar's LNG remains under force majeure to end-December.
- **Vulnerability:** crude has a ship-to-ship shuttle around the strait; gas does not. A failed deal plus a cold snap finds no buffer.

### What held, and what moved quietly.
- **No written reply reported.** No written reply to Vance's letter has been reported or acknowledged by Washington; on 31 October Rezaei, the security council secretary, said he had "not yet answered it". Speaker Ghalibaf told parliament on 3 November that Iran "has delivered a strong, rational response to US side", without saying what, how or whether in writing (claimed).
- **"The ball is in America's court":** the foreign ministry (2 Nov) and Speaker Ghalibaf (3 Nov) used the same line, and First Vice-President Aref called US messages "an acknowledgement of our victory".
- **Qatar:** a third Qatari team in a week flew to Tehran on 2 November over a draft "interim agreement" (reported). Iran refuses to send its uranium abroad.
- **China convened the region on 2 November.** It met Iran's deputy security chief and the Saudi national security adviser (carrying a letter from the crown prince), and Xi received Oman's sultan. A Chinese envoy tours all three this month.
- **The order gap:** Iran wants Hormuz and the blockade settled first and nuclear talks after ("Our conditions must be met… Nuclear talks can begin afterward", a senior official to Reuters, 3 Nov); Vance wants a "firm" and "verifiable" nuclear commitment first. Axios reports a three-day sequence of confidence-building steps "awaiting feedback from both nations".
- **Still holding:**
  - No Iranian missile salvo at any state (~54 days).
  - No strike on the nuclear sites (14th window).
  - No Hezbollah or Israel–Iran fire.
  - Mojtaba Khamenei unseen, day 249.

### The Signal You Might Miss
The calm in physical oil and Iran's new blacklist are on a collision course.

Monday's fall rests partly on Bloomberg's finding that Gulf exporters shipped near pre-war volumes in October. Some of those barrels move on the producers' own state fleets: KOTC, Bahri, ADNOC. On 2 November Iran put 23 of those ships on its non-compliance list (reported), under threat of "fines, detention, or confiscation". The night before, a tanker out of Kuwait was disabled, reportedly by drones; a link is not shown.

In the graph, the new `strait-of-hormuz` enforcement watch sits on edges already at 10.0: `→ shipping-tankers` and `→ brent-crude`. If Iran acts on that list against a producer's own ship, the "plentiful" reading fails without any US decision. Iran has now put part of the fleet behind that near-pre-war figure on its list.
