## I. What Happened

### The de-escalation stalled: the Iran–Oman route never bridged to a deal, and the US turned the screws economically instead.
The optimism of the last brief has reversed. The 5 August Iran–Oman shipping-route agreement did not convert into the US–Iran deal Trump wanted — **negotiations are deadlocked**, and rather than close, Trump *hardened*: he declared the US has "total control" of Hormuz and on 10 August added a new precondition, demanding Iran pay "compensation" before talks (CNN). With the assessment shifting to "the military campaign has failed to force the regime to capitulate," Washington pivoted to an economic vise: **a US naval blockade has brought Iranian oil exports to a near-halt** — the Kharg Island terminals sit empty — layered with new sanctions imposed on 7 August (Eurasia Business News, JPost, Iran International). Critically, this is *economic*, not kinetic: **no new US strikes on Iran were confirmed this window**, so the strike pause on the bombing campaign appears to hold, and the nuclear firebreak remains intact — the US chose to starve Iran's oil revenue rather than bomb it. Iran, for its part, has not capitulated despite its exports cut to near-zero, which raises the graph's key new risk: a cornered Iran has *more* incentive, not less, to re-escalate the chokepoint.

### The Fed cut is now locked in — July inflation came in soft, and gold hit a fresh record.
For the reader's portfolio, the confirmation matters as much as the reversal. **July US CPI, released 12 August, printed soft: +0.1% on the month, 3.4% annual (down from June), core +0.2%** (CNBC/BLS) — removing the last obstacle to the dovish pivot the last brief flagged. Rate markets now treat a **25bp September cut as a foregone conclusion, and price ~95% odds of follow-up cuts in October and December** — a full easing cycle. **Gold responded with a fresh record, ~$4,453 (+10% on the month), silver +14%** — and, tellingly, on a day the war *re-escalated* economically, confirming gold is trading on the Fed and rates, not as a war haven. The one caveat: with inflation still at 3.4% and a hawkish Fed chair (Warsh), the cut is labour-market-driven, and the 11 September CPI print — the last before the FOMC — is the residual risk to the "foregone conclusion." For emerging markets and India, a US easing cycle is a structural tailwind.

### The signal of the week is a split screen: equity fear at pre-war lows, while the shipping market prices a worsening war.
This is where the graph earns its keep. On the paper side, the market prices calm — **the VIX has fallen to 14.59, near its pre-war low**, US equities sit near records, and gold's record is a rates story. On the physical side, the war is *re-intensifying*: **the benchmark supertanker rate for the Gulf-to-China route surged to roughly $498,000 a day** (near the all-time high, about ten times normal), **war-risk insurance for Hormuz transits holds at 7.5–10% of hull value** ($10–21M per voyage) and has never normalized, and the IEA now flags a **1.8 million-barrel-a-day global shortfall** this quarter as the blockade removes Iranian barrels (Insurance Day, Briefs, IEA). The equity market believes the economic standoff is contained; the shipping and oil markets, which pay real money on real losses, price a genuine and deepening supply disruption. One of them is mispriced — and it is almost certainly the equity calm, because a VIX of 14.6 is not pricing the tail the graph is now flagging: an Iranian kinetic re-escalation of the strait as the export halt bites.

### India got more complicated — its own inflation jumped to a 19-month high, and a Tata Sons governance shock hit the market.
The "strongest setup of the cycle" the last brief described has frayed. Three headwinds returned at once: oil re-armed back above $85 (import-bill pressure); **India's own July CPI rose to a 19-month high of 4.45%** (food-driven, with some economists now flagging a possible RBI *hike* by December), a shift from the RBI's dovish tone of a week ago; and a domestic governance shock — **Tata Sons chairman N Chandrasekaran resigned on 12 August** ahead of the group's AGM, sending **TCS down 4–5%** and dragging the IT index, leaving India's largest conglomerate with leadership uncertainty (Forbes India, Bloomberg). Offsetting all of it is the confirmed US Fed easing cycle, a real capital-flow tailwind, and the **rupee held firm at ₹95.43**. The net is a market consolidating around 24,400 — flat, not falling, but no longer the clean one-way setup. Medium-term the India case is intact; the near-term is now a two-sided tape.

### Defense has finally turned the war into a durable equity bid.
One quieter but structurally important move: **Lockheed Martin is up nearly 18% on the month**, on its Q2 beat and a **record $230 billion order backlog** (a $64bn year-on-year jump, book-to-bill 3.2) and a raised full-year guide (Bloomberg, Benzinga). Unlike earlier in this cycle, when defense stocks conspicuously *lagged* the war, the demand is now showing up in contracted backlog — driven by the Iran conflict *plus* Ukraine resupply *plus* a European NATO spending ramp. That makes roughly 60% of the move a structural, multi-year story that would not reverse on an Iran ceasefire, and about 40% the war itself. Defense has become one of the cleaner ways the conflict is expressing in equities.

The equity market has decided the shift from bombing to blockade is a *de-escalation*; the shipping market has decided it is an *escalation*, and the shipping market is the one transacting against real losses. **A supertanker rate near $498,000 a day and war-risk cover stuck at 7.5–10% are not the prices of a war that is ending** — they are the prices of a supply disruption that is deepening, with the IEA now calling an outright 1.8 mb/d shortfall. The blockade that starved Iran's oil revenue is precisely the kind of cornering that has historically preceded chokepoint escalation, and a VIX of 14.6 leaves convexity extraordinarily cheap into that tail. The paper-physical divergence has been the cycle's most reliable tell; it has now inverted, and it is the equity calm that looks mispriced.
