## I. What Happened

### The war now runs through two chokepoints and a burning pipeline — and oil is back above $100 as a supply fact, not a premium.
The 13 August brief described an *economic* escalation with the strike pause holding and Brent at $87. That regime lasted seventeen days. On 30 August US forces struck IRGC mine-rocket launchers on Larak Island; Iran answered on US bases in Jordan and the UAE; and Washington adopted a **"tanker for tanker" policy** — striking Iranian tankers for Iranian fire on shipping — which it has executed three times (1, 5 and 9 September; eight-plus tankers destroyed, the *Riesco* sunk; Jordan intercepted 18 of 20 Iranian ballistic missiles on 9 September, and CBS reports an A-10 lost a wing at Muwaffaq Salti). Iran's IRGC claims hits on a US carrier and destroyer; the US denies any warship was struck. Then, inside 48 hours last week, two things the graph had watched for months happened at once: **drones launched from Iraq's Maysan province burned pumping stations on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline** — its only Hormuz bypass, 4–5 mb/d, roughly 4% of world supply — and Riyadh shut it on 11 September (Reuters sources: repairs "partial" to "five to six weeks"; Yanbu holds only 5–7 days of export stock, per OilPrice); and **the Houthis captured Perim island, Mocha and Dhubab, completing a territorial hold on the Bab el-Mandeb** — the strait every Yanbu cargo to Asia must transit. Baghdad confirmed the launch origin and sacked the Maysan commander; no group claimed the attack; Trump called Iran "probably responsible"; Tehran has said nothing. **Brent crossed $100 on 9 September for the first time since July, reached an intraday high of $110 on Friday before settling $104.61 on a talks headline, and is back at $107–108 this morning (+23% on the month; WTI $102)** after Oman postponed the one diplomatic meeting on the calendar. Hormuz transits are down to seven a day (Kpler), against ~125 pre-war.

### The Fed flipped from a "foregone" cut to an 83–85%-priced HIKE for Wednesday — and gold, tellingly, did nothing.
The August brief's central macro claim — that a September cut was locked in and a full easing cycle would carry emerging markets — inverted in two steps. At Jackson Hole Chair Warsh said the Fed still had "work to do"; then **August CPI (BLS, 11 Sep) printed +0.4% on the month, 3.4% annual, with energy +16.3% year-on-year, gasoline +27% and fuel oil +52%** — gasoline alone was a third of the monthly rise, while core sits at just 2.4%. Rate markets now price a **25bp hike on 16 September at ~83–85% (zero odds of a cut), ~91% by October and ~99% by December — some 54bp of tightening**; the 10-year has climbed from 4.64% to 4.97%. The Fed is, in other words, tightening into an oil supply shock, and because core is quiescent the entire hike path is an oil bet: it would collapse as fast as the cut path did if crude fell. The market's verdict on what this war *is* comes from gold: **flat on the month at $4,372** — 2.4% below its late-August high — through a $20 oil spike, a sunk tanker and a proxy seizing a second chokepoint. The August record was a Fed-cut bid; the hike took it away on the very days the war escalated. The dollar is only 0.6% weaker, so this is real yields: the market prices the conflict as an inflation event, not a systemic one. A Fed *hold* on Wednesday is the one thing that would flip that regime.

### Diplomacy is formally off: contact cut, mirror-image "compensation" demands, and the Oman channel stalled on Saturday night.
Vice-President Vance said on 3 September that Washington has **cut contact with Iran** for as long as it fires on ships (reported by Haaretz; no second outlet carries the statement, though it is on the record and uncontradicted). The August deadlock's binary — would Trump accept an Iran-controlled route, or Iran his compensation demand? — has resolved as *neither*: Foreign Minister Araghchi told Tasnim there can be no talks "until… the United States compensates" for violating the June memorandum, the exact inverse of Trump's 10-August precondition. Trump's own line has moved from "not looking for" talks (9 Sep) to "Tehran wants to make a deal so bad" (13 Sep) — and, crucially, to a *date*: "this war will end immediately after our election," 3 November. Iran's only live channel is Oman, with which the IRGC announced a revenue-sharing arrangement for the strait on 26 August; but the Gulf ministers' meeting Iran convened in Salalah for today — to regionalise that lane without Washington — was **postponed by Oman on Saturday "in the interests of consensus"** after Bahrain refused to attend and Saudi Arabia filed amendments (Bloomberg, AP; Tehran says the delay was "at the request of some regional countries"). Trump's view of the Gulf track: "I don't care. That's up to them." One single-source report belongs alongside this: the *New York Times* (13 Sep, unnamed Iranian and IRGC officials) says the July ship attacks that killed the June deal were launched without the knowledge of Pezeshkian, IRGC chief Vahidi or the security council, on the initiative of hardline cleric Hossein Taeb — and that Mojtaba Khamenei has not been seen since 28 February. If that holds, no Iranian institution can currently deliver a ceasefire at sea even if one were negotiated.

### India's August setup has inverted: the Fed tailwind is a headwind, the rupee had its worst week since May, and today's CPI prints into a closed market.
The "strongest setup of the cycle" of early August has become a defensive tape. **Foreign investors pulled ₹13,138 crore from Indian equities in the first eight sessions of September** (NSDL), after net inflows in July–August, taking 2026's outflow to ₹2.37 trillion — already more than the whole of 2025. The rupee, which had firmed to 94.49 on the August thesis, fell 1.2% last week to 95.69 (95.54 in thin trade today) with the RBI defending; the 10-year gilt is at a three-month high of 6.96%; **the Nifty closed Friday at 23,398, down 4.1% on the month and 4.8% over five weeks**, with 14 of 16 sectors lower — the DII wall holding Bank Nifty to −1.8%. Two things the reader must have in view for Tuesday: a Mint poll puts **August CPI at ~4.9%, a 20-month high and the third month above target**, which turns the RBI's 7-October meeting into hold-or-hike; the print lands at 17:30 IST today with the market shut, so Tuesday's open absorbs CPI, $108 Brent and the FOMC set-up in one gap. The 21-August waiver that would have let state refiners buy discounted Iranian crude was not extended — the June memorandum it hung on expired on 17 August, replaced by Treasury's "Operation Economic Outcast" secondary-sanctions programme, which names India among Iran's key partners. India's diplomatic lane held its shape: New Delhi hosted the BRICS summit with Xi, Putin and Pezeshkian this weekend and produced a declaration of "deep concern" that did not name the United States; and on Saturday the MEA condemned the attack on the tanker *MT El Gaia* off Oman — 14 Indian crew, one still missing — as "unacceptable." Trackk's Vedant Gupte has it right on flows: "September selling is a dollar-and-crude story, not an India story." The CPI leg is India's own.

### Supertanker rates doubled in a month to ~$1m a day, while defense stocks fell 12% — the physical market and the political market are pricing two different wars.
The physical war premium the August brief flagged at "$498,000 a day" has doubled: the benchmark **TD3C Gulf-to-China rate printed ~$982,000/day on Friday, and Clarksons puts it above $1 million** — 130 earning days now equal the value of a ten-year-old VLCC. Frontline is up 24% on the month. The mechanism is the one the graph carries: seven transits a day, Iranian night attacks on the "safe" Omani corridor (the FT reports the US has cut guaranteed air-defence cover to two daily time slots because darkness transits "have not proven to be the most secure"), and the Yanbu outage removing the only alternative loading point. Nobody has produced a fresh Gulf war-risk premium print this month — the last hard number is 7.5–10% of hull — which is the one gap in this brief; every proxy points up. Against this, **Lockheed and the ITA defense ETF are each down 12.3% since 13 August**, giving back the post-earnings surge, on Rubio's 25-August "strikes to sanctions" message and Trump's dated end to the war — the equity market pricing the administration's *calendar* while tankers price the *battlefield*. About 60% of that move is the Iran-specific premium coming out; the NATO/Ukraine backlog leg is untouched. The signal is the same one the graph called in August, inverted: then equities were calm and shipping was alarmed; now shipping is right and equities are trading a political promise.

### The nuclear firebreak held an eighth window; Lebanon is a ground war; Israel's election is a dead heat.
None of the three September strike cycles touched Fordow, Natanz, Esfahan or Pickaxe Mountain — Al Jazeera finds Pickaxe "largely untouched" — even as Trump said the US may strike it "very soon" (4–5 Sep) and CNN reported a "surge in construction" there; Iran's military HQ warned that bombing it "would amount to a renewal of war." The threats are rhetoric; the withholding is fact, and it remains the clearest escalation rung Washington has not climbed. In Lebanon the July "firewall" is gone in substance: Israel took the Ali al-Taher ridge (3 Sep) and demolished a two-kilometre Hezbollah tunnel complex beneath it (10 Sep); Lebanon counts 4,362 killed since March; talks have slipped to October. Israel has still not struck Iran. Its 27 October election is tied — Yashar 23.6 seats to Likud 21.8, blocs 53.6 to 53.4 (10 Sep five-poll average), neither at 61.
