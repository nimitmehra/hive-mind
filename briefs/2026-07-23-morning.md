## I. What Happened

### The Red Sea firewall went kinetic — the Houthis struck two Saudi tankers, one ablaze.
After roughly fifteen days of enforcing their Saudi-shipping embargo by email and radio, the Houthis crossed to an actual strike: two Saudi crude tankers, ***Encelia*** and ***Layla***, hit by missiles and drones, one set ablaze, all crew reported safe. This is confirmed, not a one-sided claim — **Saudi state news and UKMTO both corroborate**, alongside Reuters, NYT and Al Jazeera. It is the first Houthi ship-strike of the campaign, and it is part of what drove oil's charge overnight. But read the calibration and the limits precisely, because they are the intelligence: tankers not warships, crew safe, no mass casualty; **Bab el-Mandeb is not closed** — ISW counts thirty vessels transiting on Tuesday, *down from sixty-four*, a halving rather than a shut strait; **Saudi Arabia has not retaliated militarily**, its "vows to protect shipping" still unconverted; and a sanctioned Iranian vessel continues through the Red Sea untouched. This is targeted coercion of Saudi cargo, not a general closure — and Trump has *threatened* to send heavy bombers against the Houthis, not yet done so.

### The US posture swung hard to campaign — B-1 bombers surging, Rubio declaring the ceasefire "killed" — but the nuclear tripwire held a fourth window.
No formal decision yet, three days into "within days," but every vector moved toward escalation overnight. **Rubio pronounced the ceasefire dead** — Iran's shipping attacks "kill the deal," though "the door to diplomacy stays open" — the clearest US burial of the 10-day proposal yet, pinned to the tankers. Trump said Iran is "not ready to make a deal." The build-up is physical: **the WSJ reports the US surging forces "to expand the Iran war," with B-1 supersonic bombers deploying** — the platform that could reach Pickaxe Mountain. Yet **strike night 12 hit missile, drone and air-defence sites — and Fordow, Natanz, Esfahan, Pickaxe and the Bushehr reactor all remain untouched**, the war's firebreak intact for a fourth window running even as the bombers that could break it arrive. Iran's restraint is unchanged and remains the strongest non-weaponisation signal: no NPT-withdrawal threat, no enrichment escalation. Congress resolved its fight — **the House passed the $1.15 trillion defence bill 213-207 on a party-line vote**, made a referendum on the war; Hegseth put its cost at $37.5bn.

### Oil is charging toward $100 — a second straight 3% day — while the VIX falls to 16.64.
Brent rose 3% to **$96.93 (the ~12:42 IST print; extending past $97.7 into the afternoon), up 25.8% on the month** — a second consecutive war-restart high — as the escalation inputs stacked: night 12, the B-1 surge, Rubio's "ceasefire killed," the IRGC declaring Hormuz "completely closed" and shifting to mine-laying, and the Red Sea going kinetic. The flat price, not just refined-product cracks, is now doing the work, and Goldman's $120 conditional is back in the headlines. The discipline check still applies — Brent is **−7.7% over three months**, a re-arm *toward* not through the spring peaks — but at $97 the market is half-way to Goldman's tail and knocking on the graph's sustained-$100 trigger. **The binary is now sharply asymmetric: a joint-campaign decision plausibly gaps this through $100; only a ceasefire acceptance, which Rubio just pronounced dead, collapses it.** And the divergence is at its extreme: **the VIX fell to 16.64, toward pre-war levels, on the day oil charged to a war high and a second chokepoint went kinetic.**

### India's DII wall cracked — the domestic bid itself sold, and the war led the tape lower a third day.
For the first time this cycle the buffer that absorbed every war gap-down gave way: on 22 July **both foreign *and domestic* institutions were net sellers** — FIIs −₹819 cr, DIIs −₹418 cr, the second straight session of DII selling. That is why the decline, still orderly at around −0.6%, has become *persistent* rather than instantly bought: the Nifty closed Tuesday at ~23,996 and is a third day down, below 24,000. Three drivers overlap — oil near $100 into a June import bill already up 48%; **Trump's phased 200% generic-pharma tariff** (now the lead domestic driver, Indian pharma −3-4% and "seeking engagement" with Washington); and the **24 July trade cliff, one day out and unsigned** — the US calls the *interim* tranche "almost complete," but a US official puts the *full* deal three-to-four months away, with the pharma tariff live friction in the room. The **rupee held its record — defended, not broken:** ~96.48–96.54, the RBI holding the line with $20.72bn of borrowed dollars and cutting its record ₹-equivalent forward book rather than spending spot reserves. Tomorrow it faces a triple event — the tariff cliff, the reserves print that will flatter that borrowed-dollar defence, and Dr Reddy's and Cipla reporting into the tariff overhang.

### The ECB is set to pause, citing Middle East risk — the first central-bank confirmation of the stagflation frame.
The ECB's decision lands at ~16:45 IST, just after this brief; the reporting (Reuters, Anadolu, France 24) anchors an expected hold explicitly to the war — "cautious amid Middle East risks," with **September flagged as the decision point and the debate framed as a *hike*, not a cut**, even as TTF gas printed a fresh cycle high (€63.75) on the continued absence of Qatari LNG. If it lands as trailed, this is the first central-bank confirmation of the thesis the graph has carried for two cycles — the oil shock underwritten as a *September* problem. It pairs with the Fed, where July hike odds sit near 7.6% even at Brent $97. Both central banks are deferring a genuine supply shock to Q3.

### Two quieter but real developments: a surprise US–Saudi nuclear pact, and Pezeshkian publicly elevating the invisible successor.
Washington announced a **US–Saudi nuclear cooperation pact** — the same window Saudi tankers burned — binding Riyadh closer to the US under fire and, notably, *alarming Israel*, whose anxiety has flipped from being dragged into the war to being diplomatically sidelined ("Israel is Losing Its Seat at the Table," per Haaretz). And in Tehran, **President Pezeshkian publicly described "Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei" as taking a "hands-on role"** — the civilian president openly manufacturing authority for a successor who has not shown his face in four and a half months and whose father's memorial, due today, drew no coverage at all. Meanwhile Hezbollah's silence toward Israel is now ~16 days, Israel still has not struck Iran, and Iraqi militias stayed home — the containment holding everywhere except the Red Sea.
