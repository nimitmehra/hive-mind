## I. What Happened

### Iran killed American soldiers for the first time since March — and the war's defining statistic is gone.
Iranian missiles and drones struck **Muwaffaq Salti air base at Al-Azraq, Jordan** on Friday night. CENTCOM announced Saturday that **two US service members were killed and one is missing**; the cumulative toll is now 16 dead, 430+ wounded. Every brief since February could say Iran had inflicted zero confirmed American casualties across strikes on six states; that is over. The significance is the *calibration*, not the number: Iran's 28 June counter-strike was engineered to kill no Americans, and that restraint bought the stand-down which saved the deal. Whether this failure was deliberate is the most important open question here — and one tell suggests not. **The IRGC claimed the base and claimed destroying aircraft, but no Iranian outlet publicised the American dead**, the same de-confliction architecture as June. CENTCOM's language nonetheless shifted from "degrade" to "swiftly punish." (All six outlets carrying the casualty numbers trace to CENTCOM — one source. The aircraft claim is unverified.)

### Iran's power grid is down 4,200 MW — and the tripwire that should have pulled did not fire.
Both rungs of Trump's ultimatum — bridges and power plants — now have damage on the ground, though Washington has claimed neither. Bridges, two tunnels and a rail bridge were destroyed in Hormozgan, severing Bandar Abbas from Tehran; and strikes hit an electricity-and-desalination plant, with **Iran's Energy Ministry acknowledging "attacks on power infrastructure" for the first time** and its electricity chief reporting a **4,200 MW loss across 2,000+ damaged points**. Trump had threatened bridges and power plants four times since March without following through, and damage has now appeared on both — but note the gap in the record: **neither target set appears in any CENTCOM release, and CENTCOM has neither confirmed nor denied hitting them.** The damage is confirmed; the intent is not. Models treating his escalation threats as pure bluff should still be repriced, but not on the assumption that this was the threat being deliberately executed. **But the finding that matters more: striking Iran's power network was reportedly the precondition for a standing Iranian order to the Houthis to close Bab el-Mandeb — and the Houthis have not moved.** No UKMTO incident, no closure, no launch at Israel. Either the threshold means the national grid rather than a distribution network, or the order is unspent leverage, or Sana'a is declining. Markets seem to agree closure isn't imminent: the **Suez Canal Authority raised transit surcharges on 15 July** — laden tankers 25%→37% — which no port authority does expecting volumes to vanish. (The Iranian instruction remains one Reuters story citing three unnamed sources, uncorroborated in three days.)

### A safeguarded nuclear site was struck for the first time — at a target chosen for how little it matters.
Projectiles hit the **construction site of the Darkhovin nuclear plant** in Khuzestan early Sunday. Iran attributes the strike to the United States; **Washington has not acknowledged it, and Darkhovin appears in no CENTCOM target list** — so the strike is corroborated but its authorship rests on Tehran's word alone. Iran's atomic agency called it a "terrorist attack" on a facility "fully subject to IAEA safeguards"; the IAEA assessed **no radiological risk**, the site being barely out of the ground. Friday's brief noted Washington was deliberately *not* touching the nuclear complex. If the attribution holds, that restraint has been partially abandoned within 36 hours of the first American deaths — and choosing a target with no enriched material is exactly what makes it read as a **signal rather than a strike**, the cheapest way to put the nuclear file on the board. Trump simultaneously said the dead had made the "ultimate sacrifice" to stop an Iranian bomb, relocating the war's justification after months of fighting about shipping lanes. **Fordow, Natanz and Esfahan remain untouched; that is the real tripwire, and it is closer than Friday.** Note what Tehran did *not* do: no NPT withdrawal threat, no enrichment escalation — the AEOI invoked safeguards *in its own favour*, which is a restraint signal, not a weaponisation one.

### Brent jumped 4.6% to $88.10 — on Kuwaiti infrastructure, not American blood.
Oil closed its best week since April, up 14%, after Iran struck **Kuwait's power-and-desalination plants on two consecutive days plus the al-Ahmadi oil facilities**, host-confirmed, with the ministry ordering demand conservation. That reprices risk from transit disruption to *production-infrastructure* disruption in a country where ~90% of drinking water is desalinated. The timing matters more than it looks: **the American deaths were announced Saturday, after the close**, so Friday's price contains none of them. This market is pricing Gulf infrastructure, not US casualties — diagnostic of what it actually fears, and a reminder the casualty news is unpriced. The discipline check on the bulls is the three-month column: **Brent is −7.7% over three months** despite a declared closure, a blockade and eight nights of bombing, and energy equities badly lagged (XLE +1.2% against crude's +4.6%) — the market voting this is a temporary premium, not a durable supply loss. Underneath, refining holds the real damage: **the US diesel crack settled above $91 a barrel Thursday, an all-time record**, and Friday's weakness in heating oil and gasoline was profit-taking in the most stretched margin in history, not demand destruction.

### The July Fed hike has been priced out — and that, not the war, is why gold rose.
Odds of a hike at the 28–29 July FOMC **collapsed from 46.5% on 13 July to about 13.3% by Friday** — removing one of the two binaries flagged for the coming ten days. This is not a dovish turn: **~73% odds of at least one hike by December remain**, and Chair Warsh used his first congressional testimony to pledge a policy "regime change" and declare "no tolerance for persistently elevated inflation," while becoming the first Chair to submit nothing to the dot plot — raising rate volatility by design. The hike moved *out*, not away. The consequence showed up in the metal: **gold rose 0.8% on a day oil rose 4.6%**, which the inverted-haven regime says shouldn't happen, until you see the 10-year fall to 4.54%. The regime is intact and more extreme than ever on the month — **gold −7.2% over 30 days and −16.4% over three, silver −19.4%, platinum −11.0%** — the entire precious complex de-rating through an escalating war. Only the front-end transmission loosened.

### Gas is where this war is actually being won — and Europe has conceded the winter.
Oil has buffers; gas has none. TTF is at **€57–59/MWh, +29–42% in three weeks**, and Asian JKM leapt **18.5% in a single day** to $19.93/MMBtu. The physical picture is worse: **EU storage is 53% full against a 75% five-year norm — the lowest in fifteen years** — with July injections at a six-year low and net injections since April covering only 40% of winter need. Brussels has already relaxed its mandatory target from 90% to 80%, which is a regulator conceding before winter begins. Two mechanisms keep it self-reinforcing. Qatar's loss is not a spike — **12.8 mtpa, 17% of its system, is offline for three to five years**, force majeure is in its fourth month, and QatarEnergy *paused* its restart after its own tanker was hit, so the constraint is Hormuz safety, not repairs. And **US LNG is being pulled away from Europe by Asia**: 41.9% of American exports went to Europe in June, the first sub-50% reading in roughly two years. Europe's shortfall and Asia's bid are the same trade. The **ECB meets Thursday** on June projections that put Q3 energy inflation at 12.5% — made before TTF broke €57, and not revised at this meeting.

### India acted on the war for the first time — against Tehran, five days before a tariff cliff with Washington.
The **MEA summoned Iran's deputy ambassador** over attacks on seafarers, and the shipping regulator issued **Circular 36, barring Indian seafarers from any vessel transiting Hormuz.** That second is not symbolic: **Indians are ~9–10% of the world's seafarers, concentrated in tanker crews**, so a blanket withdrawal genuinely constrains manning ships through the strait. The direction is the intelligence — **India protested to Tehran and has not protested to Washington**, condemning strikes on American bases without naming Iran. That tilt sits alongside a trade deal **unsigned at T−5**: the deadline is now resolved as **24 July**, the lapse of a 10% Section 122 tariff imposed for 150 days on 24 February, and five days of ministerial silence into a statutory cliff is itself the finding. One non-consensus note — the cliff may run *downward*, since that 10% sits on top and MFN reversion is 0% for generic pharmaceuticals. Meanwhile equities refused to care: the **Nifty rose 1.1% to 24,334, up 1.0% on the month** through a war restart, carried by a Saturday bank sweep — ICICI +15.9%, Axis +22.5%, Kotak +26% — all with improving asset quality. The macro is the opposite story, and India's real Hormuz exposure has moved: **~70% of its crude now bypasses the strait, but LPG and LNG imports run 50% below pre-conflict levels**, hitting subsidised cooking gas and fertiliser feedstock.

## II. What Analysts Say

At $88.10 on day eight of a declared closure, the market is pricing a reopening Goldman is not.

### The Reuters survey (31 forecasters, ~17 July) — and the contradiction it creates
Consensus **cut** 2026 Brent to **$84.50 from $90.44** — in the week the war restarted and the strait was declared closed — assuming traffic normalises. Set against **Polymarket pricing Hormuz normalisation by 31 August at 12%**, consensus and the prediction market flatly contradict each other. One is badly wrong, and the trade lives in that gap. (The EIA's $74 Q3 outlook is dead.)

### Lloyd's Market Association / Lloyd's List — why the strait stays shut
Safety, not insurance *availability*, is driving reduced traffic: cover is still written, owners won't sail. But per-transit underwriting capacity has fallen from $2–3bn to **$500m–$1.5bn** and **six of thirteen P&I clubs have withdrawn Gulf war cover**. **Falsifiable:** a ceasefire followed by transits recovering in days rather than weeks breaks the thesis.

### The Soufan Center — reliable on structure, weak on timelines
Both sides "believe they have leverage" and have "misjudge[d] the other's resolve." Their call that Iran's new leadership is drawn from the IRGC rather than the clergy was **corroborated this weekend by Araghchi's admission that he has never met the Supreme Leader**; their GCC-fissure call is **holding** — Abu Dhabi's *The National* published "weakened regime" language while Saudi outlets went quiet.

### Source Tone Assessment
**The most important tonal finding reverses Friday's read: Iran's elite has not converged on the hawks — it is in an open legitimacy fight.** CNN's Tehran-sourced reporting has ultra-hardliners accusing Ghalibaf and Pezeshkian of a "coup" against a Supreme Leader nobody can reach, a security-linked figure publicly threatening the president's life without consequence, and Araghchi pelted with rocks as a "traitorous sellout." That reframes Friday's "keep your word or pay the price" as domestic survival posturing rather than a state escalation decision — a more tractable picture, not a worse one. **Washington moved the other way: Fox re-hardened into war mobilisation**, closing the crack in administration messaging; per the standing rule that Fox leads policy, no de-escalation pivot is signalled. **The loudest silence is Congressional** — nothing, despite the first combat deaths; the casualties suppressed dissent rather than triggering it. **The Gulf crossed a legal threshold**, its secretary-general calling Iran's strikes "war crimes requiring international accountability," yet nobody counter-escalates — and **Oman, the mediator, went completely silent through the worst weekend of the war.**

## III. What the Graph Tells Me

### Cascade Watch
Fifty-four of 63 nodes moved — but **the headline is that not one trigger escalated.** Across a window containing the first American deaths, a strike on a safeguarded nuclear site and both threatened rungs, all eighteen reviewed triggers held, because in every case the written threshold went unmet. The hardest call was `houthis`: the **Bab el-Mandeb precondition fired** — the US struck Iranian power infrastructure and Tehran admitted it — yet it stays at *watching*, because the condition requires a confirmed attack verified by maritime trackers, and there was none. A fired precondition plus an absent response is not an attack; moving it would have been a more sophisticated version of the March 2026 Houthi error. The `iran` rial trigger is now the closest un-fired threshold in the graph — **an intraday 1,953,500, just 2.4% below 2,000,000** — and still did not fire, because the session closed lower and the graph does not fire on approaches. On the market side `fed→gold` took its fifth inverted-haven confirmation at weight 10, and `qatar→european-ttf-gas` rose 7.0→9.5. Two edges were deliberately *cut*: **`brent-crude→india` from 10 to 8.5**, because ~70% of India's crude now bypasses Hormuz; and **`fii-flows→nifty-50` from 10 to 7.0**, because Friday's selling collapsed 91% while the index rose, and the relationship is now asset-class-specific — foreigners bought over $4.2bn of Indian *bonds* while selling equity. A third correction matters for fertiliser: **urea de-escalated ~50% on Chinese export quotas reopening, not on anything Iranian**, so a new `china→fertilizer-urea` edge now carries it, and phosphate, not nitrogen, is the tight leg.

### The Signal You Might Miss
**A ceasefire will not reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and almost nothing is priced for that lag.** The consensus model treats the strait as diplomatically gated — settle the war, ships sail. The data says it is *underwriting*-gated. Price is already clearing: VLCC fixtures printed at **$469,000 and $439,000 a day**, and a charterer earning that absorbs a 5% war-risk premium without blinking. What is not clearing is capacity — **per-transit underwriting capacity has fallen from $2–3bn to $500m–$1.5bn, and six of thirteen P&I clubs have withdrawn Gulf war cover.** A ship without P&I cannot enter most ports or satisfy its charterparty, so this is a hard *quantity* cap no freight rate can lift. Underwriters restore capacity on observed loss experience — weeks of clean running, not a signing ceremony. **Expect four to eight weeks between any settlement and a real recovery in transits** — and India has just added a third constraint, removing ~9–10% of the world's tanker crews from the route. One artefact for modellers: the published TD3C index sits at $286,500/day against those $469k fixtures, because the Baltic panel has too few compliant fixtures to assess. **Anyone pricing Hormuz off TD3C is working with a 40% discount to reality.**

### Risk Landscape
**Intensifying.** The nuclear rung — Fordow, Natanz and Esfahan are the tripwire, and Washington has surged refuelling tankers and radar-suppression aircraft to Israel while Axios and the WSJ report Trump weighing further nuclear targets. And Iran's economy, now failing faster than its military: **inflation is 88.6%, not the 69% this graph carried until tonight**; the rial is 2.4% from its threshold; and **China's Iranian crude imports have collapsed to 556,000 bpd, the lowest since January 2023**, with Shandong teapots buying 16–20m barrels from Qatar, Iraq and the UAE instead — buyers who re-paper contracts do not automatically come back.

**Stable but loaded.** Every containment firewall held through the worst weekend of the war: **Hezbollah has fired nothing for twelve days**, Israel has not struck Iran since the restart and is being actively excluded by Washington, Iraqi militias stayed home, and the Gulf states absorbed strikes on their power and water without joining anything. The most telling is diplomatic — **Lebanon's president is at the White House on Tuesday, the first such visit since 2009, while Netanyahu still cannot get a meeting.**

**Fading.** The 29 July Fed hike, now ~87% priced out. And defence as a war hedge: **ITA is −3.7% on the month and did not move on American deaths.** De-escalation will equally not rescue uranium equities (**URA −19.1% while spot uranium is flat**) or the Asian AI complex, whose bear case survives any Iran outcome.

**Deployment (India): posture stays defensive.** One binary improved this week — the Fed — and the other did not. **Do not add India risk before 24 July.** Conditions to re-engage: (1) **the trade deal signed** — the early tell is the Indian delegation flying to Washington; no travel by Tuesday reads as slippage past the cliff; (2) **FII net buying for three-plus sessions after an oil peak is in** — Friday's 91% collapse in the sell rate is necessary but not sufficient while FIIs hold 216,528 net short index futures; (3) **a mutual stand-down holding seven days**, remembering the strait then needs four to eight more weeks of underwriting. Watch the rupee, not the index: the RBI's defence of the record low lands in the reserves print on **Friday 24 July — the same day as the cliff.** Monday opens against unpriced American deaths, a nuclear precedent and two more Kuwaiti strikes, offset by the Fed and the bank results.
