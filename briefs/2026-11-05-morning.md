## I. What Happened

### Oil fell through $100 on a post from Washington, not on anything in the Gulf.
**The price.** January Brent settled at **$94.62 on Wednesday, down 8.7%**, its lowest close since 29 September. It has fallen 16.6% in three sessions and was ~$93.9 at 11:00 IST today. US crude (WTI) settled at $88.12. Headlines saying oil is "below $90" mean WTI, not Brent.

**The trigger.** By 21:00 IST Brent had slipped ~$2.4 on the election result and on Trump telling the FT, as the FT reported it, that he had no immediate plans to strike.
- **21:08 IST:** Vice-President Vance posted on X: "The reason we lost is the war. The president himself said so last night." (We fixed the time from the post's ID.)
- **The fall:** Brent dropped from $101.2 to $93.40 in two hours, ignoring bullish US inventory data. Trump later called Vance's view "largely true".
- **Our split:** about three-quarters of the fall was the war premium; the rest was automatic selling once $100 broke, a small US heating-oil loan and the Russia–Ukraine talks.

**What did not change.** There was no Iranian answer, no reopening announcement and no Saudi or OPEC+ supply signal. Tracked Hormuz crossings are lower so far in November than in October (one tracker: ~17 a day, −30%). January now sits at JPMorgan's ~$94 estimate of fair value, and ~$10 above Goldman's relayed ~$85.

### Washington argued in public; the military kept preparing; night two passed quietly.
**Vance (on the record).** He is the official who wrote to Iran's security chief. He cites a pollster's four reasons many of his 2024 voters went Democratic; the fourth is "change our relationship to Israel".

**Trump (on camera).** "If I had it to do again, I'd have to do it again because long term it's the only answer." Asked when the war ends, he said whenever he wants, "but it's got to be done right" (relays differ on the exact words). To Fox (reported): "Certainly if Iran doesn't behave, I will. And if they do… we can sign something up."

**Inside (reported).**
- **Axios, the camps:** Vance (with envoy Witkoff, in some relays) pushes for a deal; Hegseth and Netanyahu want to "finish the job".
- **Axios, the military:** "finishing preparations for possible resumption of major combat operations", with no order given.
- **WSJ:** the White House has debated whether to admit publicly to "ongoing strikes".

**The night.** No strike on Iran was reported on the second night of the window. Democratic leaders in Congress want the war ended, but they have named no bill to do it.

**What it means.** The administration's number two now calls the war a political loss, and markets traded that as its end. No change to the blockade, the preparations or the US terms has been announced.

### Tehran claimed the vote and moved nowhere; the Qatari window closed with no answer.
**The window.** The 36-hour feedback window a Qatari official gave Reuters closed at ~06:30 IST today, with no answer reported from either side. Axios says Qatar is still "trying to win" support for a draft that "could restart direct talks". Doha has said nothing since Tuesday.

**Tehran (on the record).**
- **Mohsen Rezaei (security council secretary):** Iran handed Trump "a strategic defeat on the battlefield and in the Hormuz… and a POLITICAL DEFEAT in Washington".
- **Azizi (parliament's security committee chair, reported):** "No change will occur in any of Iran's strategies until the conditions set by Iran are fulfilled."
- **No official Iranian reaction** to Vance's post was found, and there is still no reply to his letter.

**The IRGC (Revolutionary Guards).** An adviser to its commander warned that if the UAE or Bahrain let the US attack from their soil, "Our responses will no longer be proportionate or commensurate." There was no Iranian fire at any state and no retaliation for the 2-November Qeshm strike. Drones did hit Iranian Kurdish exile camps in northern Iraq on 4 November (Komala blames the IRGC), part of Iran's long-running campaign against armed exiles.

### India took the oil relief in fuel stocks and bonds; the rupee barely moved.
**Flows.** Foreign investors bought **₹7,284.03 crore** of Indian shares on Wednesday (exchange provisional data), their fourth straight buying day; domestic funds sold ₹3,712 crore, and foreigners' index-futures book is heavily net short (a hedge). Tonight's figure decides the graph's five-day test.

**Thursday, India's first session after the oil fall.** The Nifty was 24,370 at 11:10 IST (+1.7%), up 4% in three sessions and 6% in five. State fuel retailers IOC, BPCL and HPCL rose 2.7–4.7%; oil producers and standalone refiners fell 2–4%.

**The rupee.** PTI's final Wednesday close was 94.46, four paise through the cap. On Thursday it opened at 94.36 and was pushed straight back to ~94.50 by dollar buying, despite a $9 fall in oil. No report names the buyer. PTI blamed "the American currency's strength in overseas markets", although the dollar index it quoted was down 0.34%.

**Bonds and policy.** The 10-year yield fell ~11 basis points on Wednesday to ~7.19%, its best day since early April, but that came before the oil fall (on the US vote, oil's earlier drift and liquidity). The oil-fall relief is today's move to ~7.06% (a tracker). January Brent now equals the RBI's $95 assumption for the second half. Pump prices are frozen, and New Delhi has said nothing.

**Sanctions.** The US Treasury sanctioned a Chandigarh-based network (two Indian nationals and their firms) for false-flagging tankers that carry Iranian oil (4 Nov).

### Markets traded the war as ending; chips fell for a different reason.
**The war-end trade (Wednesday).**
- **Up:** the Dow +3.0%, the S&P 500 +1.6%, European shares about +2%. The VIX (Wall Street's fear gauge) fell 20% to 16.6.
- **Down:** energy shares −4.7%, Lockheed −6.9%, tanker owners −7 to −8%; European gas to ~€85.
- **Rates:** the US 10-year closed at 4.90%, its fifth straight close below 5%, mostly through lower inflation expectations, that is, cheaper oil. A December Fed hike is still ~82% priced.
- **Gold:** +3.4% to $4,073, on a weaker dollar rather than the war.

**Chips, a separate story.** Chip stocks fell 3.1% while Microsoft rose 6.7%, on expected Democratic and Texas limits on new data centres. Korea took the hit on Thursday: KOSPI −5%, SK Hynix −9.5%, foreigners −₩1.97 trillion by 13:00 Seoul time. It is not war-driven, but it pushes the same money toward India.

### Israel: no route to 61 seats; Jerusalem said almost nothing about Vance.
**The count.** President Herzog's coalition consultations opened on Wednesday with no pledged route to a 61-seat majority, which fires a graph trigger. On the parties' stated positions, Eisenkot will get 56 recommendations and Netanyahu 46. Five of the ultra-Orthodox UTJ's seven MKs will recommend no one, two are undecided, and the Joint List (11) has not decided. Most parties, Likud included, see Herzog today.

**On Vance.** Herzog was the only Israeli office-holder found on the record (Times of Israel): "sentiments that are in the air in our great friend and ally". Netanyahu's office said nothing. An Israeli official told Ynet the war is "effectively… on hold", and that the worry is a deal that reopens Hormuz and leaves out the nuclear file (reported). There was no fire between Israel and Iran.

### Saudi front: Houthi claims, Saudi silence, and a defence agreement with Syria.
**The claims.** The Houthis claim a ballistic missile scored a "direct" hit on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, plus strikes in Jazan and Najran. Saudi Arabia has not commented. Only the air-raid alerts are confirmed (the Riyadh region, Najran, Jazan; lifted within minutes), and the coalition has announced no interception for ~34 hours.

**Syria.** Saudi Arabia and Syria signed a defence cooperation agreement in Riyadh on Wednesday (terms unpublished; ~$403m of Saudi loans). The Aden front has had no wire update since 2 November, and Aramco's December prices are not out.

### What held.
- No Iranian salvo at any state (~56 days).
- No UKMTO-verified ship hit for ~34 hours. The IRGC claims two tankers and a tug on fire, unnamed and unverified.
- No strike on the nuclear sites (16th window).
- No Houthi attack on a non-Saudi ship (~55 days).
- No European government statement on the strike window.
- Seoul extended its navy unit to waters off Duqm with a mine-clearing team, for a Hormuz clearance "once hostilities end" (Assembly approval pending).
- Mojtaba Khamenei unseen, day 251.
