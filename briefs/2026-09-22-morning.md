## I. What Happened

### Oil gave back $8 in four sessions and touched $100 — on a president saying he is "open" to a meeting, a general saying the strait is flowing, and Saudi crude taking the long way round. No barrel came back.
Brent (November) fell every session from Tuesday's four-month high of $108.75 to **$100.34 on Monday (−3.4%)**, printed below $100 intraday for the first time in roughly two weeks, and posted its first weekly loss in three; it is ~$101.5 this morning. The drivers, in the order the market cited them: Trump telling Fox on Sunday he is "probably open" to meeting Pezeshkian in New York and standing down a Houthi air campaign (below); CENTCOM's Admiral Cooper announcing in a Saturday video that Hormuz oil and LNG flows are at a six-month high, primary lanes are mine-cleared and "more than a billion barrels" have left the Gulf "in the last couple months" under 2,000-plus escorts; Aramco loading roughly 14 million barrels onto seven VLCCs at once at Ras Tanura on Sunday, with satellite data showing Saudi crude moving *through* Hormuz at 2.8–2.9 mb/d, four times August's rate; and a JPMorgan note putting September "fair value" near $90 with the honest line "we simply don't know how to model the endgame." What did not change is the physical market the graph tracks: Kpler says Yanbu has loaded nothing since 11 September and that a bypass restores half of Saudi Red Sea exports "within a month," not the "days" Bloomberg and Secretary Wright guided on 16 September — that clock expires tomorrow; **Aramco has told every European term buyer its October allocation is zero** (Bloomberg), because the Yanbu–SUMED route Europe relies on is shut; Oman and Dated Brent are reported above $130, a ~$30 premium to futures and the widest paper-physical gap of the cycle. This is the third time since June that the futures screen has sold a Washington de-escalation narrative while the physical market held; twice the reversal came from Tehran. TTF gas fell 7.9% on the same read with European storage at 68%, the lowest for the date in two decades.

### Washington loaded the bombs for a Yemen campaign and then, a day after a fire at Riyadh's airport fuel farm, put them back.
Reports in the *New York Times*, corroborated in part by Axios and Reuters, say Trump told advisers on Wednesday he opposed striking the Houthis, reversed on Thursday after a call from Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and ordered the Pentagon to prepare, then cancelled at midday Sunday with target lists approved and munitions being loaded — because, per an American official, the Houthis had assured Washington through Oman that they would not attack US forces or non-Saudi shipping and strikes "would likely collapse" that understanding, and because his inner circle did not want a new front while engaged with Iran. Trump's public version on Fox: "The Houthis called us… they don't want to fight with us." The Houthis said the same on the record — Mohamed al-Bukhaiti told AP on Sunday, "We are not targeting the United States, or any other nation, in the strait, with the sole exception of the Saudi enemy" — and their behaviour matches: ten days after taking Perim there is still no UKMTO-logged attack on a merchant hull, and five laden Saudi tankers exited the Red Sea unmolested last week. Against Saudi Arabia itself they escalated: **a ballistic missile at Riyadh was intercepted at dawn on Saturday — the first Houthi attack on the Saudi capital of this war and the first Riyadh air-raid alert since the July escalation — while Reuters imagery showed black smoke from the fuel-storage area at King Khalid airport and the *Wall Street Journal* reported a strike on a jet-fuel facility there** — the coalition says the missile was destroyed and reports no damage; whether the fire was a warhead or interceptor debris is unresolved. Two days earlier drone debris in Taif produced the first Saudi civilian death since the 2022 truce. What Riyadh received instead of American strikes: State Department approval of a $24.3bn, 48-jet F-35 sale, British refuelling tankers "for several weeks," a Turkish offer of "potential military aid," and a Pakistani pledge to defend the kingdom "to any extent… physically" under a Mecca Pact that has been invoked in speeches but not formally activated. The Saudi ask goes into today's Trump–GCC meeting unanswered.

### Ships are being hit in Hormuz again — five tankers in six days, two on Monday with crew injured — and nobody has claimed a single one.
UKMTO logged an inbound tanker struck by an "unknown projectile" on Monday (two crew with minor injuries; vessel continued under its own power) and, hours later, an outbound LPG carrier hit by debris from unknown projectiles; that follows a hull breach and fire on one tanker and two other incidents between 16 and 18 September. The Joint Maritime Information Center rates the strait "SEVERE" and IRGC attack "highly likely," but UKMTO has attributed none of the five and Iran has claimed none; the IRGC's only in-window claims were four unconfirmed drone shootdowns. This is how Cooper's "six-month high" and gCaptain's count of 17 commodity transits over the weekend — against a pre-war norm of well over 100 a day — are both true: fewer, larger, escorted Saudi and Emirati VLCCs are lifting *barrels* while vessel *counts* remain roughly 90% below pre-conflict levels (UKMTO), and the projectiles are landing on the ships that do go. Tehran's political line moved the other way from the market's: Speaker Ghalibaf said on Saturday the strait "will remain closed" until Washington honours the lapsed June memorandum, and an adviser to the unseen Supreme Leader, via IRNA, said not until Trump and Netanyahu are "removed."

### Iran sent Washington seven conditions through Qatar, every institution in Tehran spoke harder than its president — and its president still boards a plane for New York today.
Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, told Al Jazeera that Iran has conveyed seven conditions for talks via Qatar and "awaits Trump's response," naming three — an end to the war on all fronts, release of frozen funds, an end to the naval blockade — and warning of "decisive war" if refused; neither Doha nor Washington has confirmed receipt, so this is one side's account of a document. It is harder than Pezeshkian's own 15 September offer, and it came from the institution that would have had to endorse him. The rest of the week's Iranian voices were harder still: the Khatam al-Anbiya headquarters said it has information the US is "preparing to resume strikes" and that any state assisting will be "complicit"; IRGC spokesman Mohebbi promised to "change the weapons and geography of the war… the world will be astonished." Trump, in the same Fox interview in which he asked "if and when do I blow the entire nation up" and listed his options as "wipe it out, let it rot with the economy, or make a deal," said he is "probably open" to meeting Pezeshkian; **no Iranian institution has acknowledged the offer** (IranWire: "Tehran has yet to react"), and Ambassador Waltz added Washington's own precondition — any negotiator "must speak for the Ayatollah" — which by construction excludes the president. Pezeshkian and Araghchi hold US visas with movement confined to UN transit and their communications team denied entry; he speaks Wednesday. The 15 September incompetence motion against him has gone quiet; Mojtaba Khamenei is 207 days unseen. The graph's "endorsed or sidelined" test resolves as neither — he has been talked past.

### India got the oil relief and none of the flow relief — and the reason the bond ignored oil is a ₹1 trillion RBI operation the graph had missed.
The rupee closed at **95.81 on Monday**, back from Thursday's 96.13 without testing the 96.60 record; the Nifty rose a fourth straight session to 23,414 (+1.3% off its 15 September low; ~23,410–23,460 live); India VIX fell 11% in three sessions to 10.9. Foreigners kept selling: NSDL shows **₹23,676 crore withdrawn through the exchanges to 19 September**, ₹1,300 crore short of the graph's ₹25,000 crore September trigger with seven sessions left, while domestic institutions bought a fifth straight week (₹36,219 crore). The 10-year did not move — 7.065% through an $8 fall in crude — because the marginal driver of Indian rates is now domestic: the RBI is selling ₹1 trillion of gilts this month (₹25,000 crore on Monday at three-times bids; the last tranche on 28 September) to drain the ₹6 lakh crore of surplus rupee liquidity created by its own FCNR(B) and special-swap mobilisation, which pulled in roughly $133–144bn. **That mobilisation is why India's reserves are $780.8bn — not the $675bn this brief has been carrying — and why last week's $4.9bn drawdown (RBI data) is intervention-consistent but not a stress number.** Citi pulled its RBI hike call forward to 7 October and SBI Research joined it; five houses now expect 25bp (HSBC, Nomura, Standard Chartered, Citi, SBI Research), and two see the 10-year at 7.25% by December.

### Equity volatility fired the graph's "de-escalation" marker in the week a capital was struck and five tankers were hit; the Nasdaq made a record on chips, not peace.
The VIX closed below 16 for a third straight session (15.44, 14.81, 14.87) — the threshold the `vix` node set in May as the de-escalation confirmation marker — and the Nasdaq rose 2.3% to a record 27,122 with the S&P +1.5% and semiconductors +4.0%. About three-quarters of Monday's rally was the AI cycle, not the war: Meta's new agent drove Intel +12%, AMD +10% to a $1trn valuation and Arm +10%; Korea reported record September chip exports ($34.1bn, more than triple a year ago), lifting the KOSPI back above 7,000; and Xi's Thursday state dinner with Nvidia's Huang and OpenAI's Altman fed tariff-truce hopes. The crisis-relief leg — oil under $100 intraday, the 10-year back to 4.96% — was the remaining quarter. Gold is $4,374: up 1% since the Fed's hike as real yields eased, down 0.9% Monday on risk-on, and unmoved by Riyadh or Hormuz — still trading the Fed, not the war. Defence barely reacted to the F-35 approval or the Pentagon's new $43.6bn war-cost tally (18 dead, 830 wounded); fertiliser fell 3–4% on Trump's Belarus potash post, which is Canada trade politics rather than Hormuz.

### The quiet threads held: no US strike ashore in at least twelve operational periods, no Iranian ballistic launch in ten-plus days, no nuclear threat named; Lebanon silent through Yom Kippur; Israel's polls unchanged; the ECB held.
The nuclear firebreak entered a tenth window with no strike on the enrichment complex and no new presidential naming of it — the "blow the entire nation up" line was generic. ### The Signal You Might Miss
The week's headline is that oil fell $8 because peace is closer. The graph's read is that **oil fell $8 because Saudi Arabia started using the chokepoint the war is about — and the market mistook the symptom for the cure.** Yanbu is still at zero; Aramco is moving 2.9 mb/d *through* Hormuz under American escort and loading seven VLCCs at a time at Ras Tanura, which is exactly the "six-month-high flows" Admiral Cooper announced. Those barrels fell out of the European market (October allocations zero) and into Asia's; they lifted Cooper's numbers, JPMorgan's inventory math and Frontline's tonne-miles; they did nothing for the 17-ship weekend transit count, the five projectiles that hit tankers in the same lane, or the ~$30 premium a refiner pays for a prompt physical cargo. The `saudi-arabia → shipping-tankers` edge the graph seeded five days ago is the mechanism, and it cuts both ways for the investor: if Yanbu restarts, tonne-miles fall and the *tanker* trade weakens even as oil eases; if Hormuz stays escorted with Yanbu shut, the tanker trade holds even as futures sell the calendar. And the three US–Iran "events" the market bought this week — "open to a meeting," seven conditions via Qatar, a cancelled strike — have one thing in common: none of them has a second party. Iran has not acknowledged the offer; Qatar has not confirmed the conditions; the strike that did not happen prevented nothing that had started. The last two times the futures screen traded Washington's words against Tehran's barrels (June, August), Tehran set the price.
