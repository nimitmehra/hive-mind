## I. What Happened

### Oil fell about 9% in two sessions, to below $90 intraday, as Trump talked down new strikes. Night seven brought no strike on Iran, but Saudi Arabia's export pipeline was hit and Houthi fire at Riyadh went on.
**The price (confirmed).**
- **Monday's settle:** January Brent settled at **$92.57**, down **5.85%**. That is the lowest front-month settle since 31 August (yfinance; Reuters: "the lowest since late August").
- **Tuesday:** Brent traded below $90 for the first time since 31 August (from about 14:20 IST). After the US inflation figures it hit a low of **$89.15**, and was **$89.54 at 19:15 IST**.
- **The curve:** the gap between January and December 2027 has narrowed to about **$13**, from $25.32 on 30 October. That is roughly half the short-term war premium gone on this measure. Diesel joined the fall on Tuesday.

**What moved it.** The links to the price below were made by the outlets named, not by us.
- **Monday:** Reuters said oil fell after Trump "downplayed the likelihood of renewed strikes on Iran", citing his Sunday line to CNN ("highly unlikely"). After the settle, Reuters added that Trump "signals restraint".
- **Tuesday:** the fall continued on Trump's "more conciliatory tone" (KCM's Tim Waterer, via Reuters). Supply news added to it: a JPMorgan estimate of a ~1.4m barrel-a-day October surplus, higher OPEC output (Reuters survey) and weak physical cargoes.
- **The timing does not fit neatly:**
  - **Trump's meeting** with Qatar's prime minister (22:30 IST Monday) left the price flat.
  - **The sharpest minutes** came at 00:53–01:12 IST, around the settle: $93.39 to $90.63.
  - **The first publication we found of every post-meeting Trump statement on Iran came after that:** a Reuters phone interview, first relayed at 02:02 IST (when Reuters conducted or first ran it is not known); a Truth Social post at 02:59 IST; and "effectively over" at ~04:02 IST. Reuters' own "signals restraint" headline ran while Brent was still more than $7 down, before that relay.
  - **So:** what traders reacted to at that moment is not established. Tuesday's further fall did come after his words.

**What Trump said (on the record).**
- **Truth Social:** Gulf states "would prefer the status quo, in other words, a continuation of the Greatest Naval Blockade in History, and the new Operation Iron Gate"; the US is "locked and loaded"; "At this point, however, that seems to be quite unlikely"; "We are seeing positive momentum toward an end to this Conflict".
- **Briefing room:** "Iran militarily is effectively over. We're just keeping it the status quo." **On C-SPAN:** "We may have to hit Pickaxe Mountain if they're gonna do something there" (the deep tunnel complex near Natanz).

**What did not stop the fall:** a confirmed hit on Saudi Arabia's main export bypass; Tehran's on-record denial of talks (both below); Iran's own state-aligned media naming an Omani island as a target (Section III); and at sea, UKMTO (the UK navy's shipping-advice centre) reclassifying Saturday's tanker fire off Khasab as an "attack", while the Revolutionary Guards claimed two more tankers halted and a mine hit (unconfirmed).

- **What it means:** the Turkish intelligence chief's claim of a Vance–Rezaei meeting in Doha "next week" now faces on-record Iranian denials and has no US or Qatari confirmation.

### Saudi Arabia confirmed an attack on its East-West oil pipeline. It caused a "limited fire", no lost flow, and no attacker was named.
- **The statement (confirmed):** an Energy Ministry source told the Saudi Press Agency on Monday evening that the line in the Madinah region suffered "a targeting that resulted in a limited fire". The fire was contained, there were no casualties, and operations were "unaffected".
- **Why it matters:** the line carries crude across the kingdom to Yanbu on the Red Sea. It is Saudi Arabia's main way around a closed Hormuz.
- **Who did it is unknown:** no Houthi or Iraqi-faction claim by Tuesday evening. When the line was hit on 10 September, Riyadh said the drones came from Iraq, and it shut for 11 days. Al Jazeera English's headline says "Houthi attack"; the Saudi statement and the article's body do not.
- **Tuesday:** the Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted three missiles and a drone; on Monday night it said it had destroyed three missile-production sites in Sanaa. It was the third day of air-raid alerts in Riyadh; the capital's schools moved online. The Houthis' claimed hit on Abqaiq, Aramco's largest processing plant, is still unconfirmed. Aramco's shares fell 1.9%, with no cause reported.

### US inflation came in hot, and the Fed's December rate rise is now fully priced. The Fed's case rests on core prices, which exclude energy; how much oil added to the headline is not known.
- **The figures (confirmed):** October consumer prices rose **0.4%** on the month (forecast 0.3%); core prices, which exclude food and energy, rose **0.3%** (forecast 0.2%). No year-on-year rate was published: the 2025 shutdown left a gap in the data. The energy component was not available to us, so we cannot say how much of the surprise was oil.
- **Before the figures:** Fed Governor Waller said in London: "Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk." If core inflation came in "around 0.3 percent or even higher", he said, "a 50 basis point hike… should be considered".
- **The move (our calculation from fed-funds futures):** December-rise odds went from ~82% on Monday to ~96–99% after Waller, then fully priced after the figures; half-point odds peaked near 18% and fell to ~7% within 15 minutes, then were back near ~16% by 19:45 IST. S&P futures fell 0.6%, Nasdaq futures 0.8%; two-year yields rose ~5–6bp (our estimate); gold fell.
- **At the US open (20:00 IST; added after the fact-check):** the S&P 500 was at 7,506.79 at ~20:15 IST (−0.6%), the Nasdaq −0.7% and the VIX 21.3; Brent ~$89.8.
- **What it means:** on Monday the two-year Treasury yield rose 5bp while oil fell 5.85%. The Fed's case is now core services, and the war barely enters it.
- **Before that:** Monday's S&P 500 rose **2.35%**, its best day since 8 April, on falling oil (CNBC); Korea's KOSPI rose **4.4%** on Tuesday. China's October crude imports hit **9.88m barrels a day**, the most since March, which supports oil; the market ignored it.
