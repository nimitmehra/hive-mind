## I. What Happened

### Oil touched $100 overnight, then crashed nearly 8% — the war premium deflating on a *reported* de-escalation, even as Trump publicly demands Iran's "unconditional surrender."
This is the day's defining move, and its shape is the intelligence. Brent topped $100 a barrel overnight (CNN, 23 July) on the stack of escalation the graph has tracked — strike night 13, the Houthi tanker strikes, Hormuz throughput collapsing to a trickle — and then **reversed to $92.71, down 7.9% intraday** by ~13:19 IST, the sharpest single-day fall of the war restart. The driver is a **reported** de-escalation that the news cycle underplayed and the oil market did not: Qatari mediators in Tehran are said to have reported "breakthroughs," a **15-point US proposal** is circulating via Pakistani intermediaries (sanctions relief, nuclear rollback, IAEA monitoring, and a Hormuz "middle corridor" between Omani and Iranian waters), and Trump is reported to have **held off on a threatened *larger* escalation** — the "massive attack" he has been weighing — all while he *publicly* declared there will be "no deal unless Iran surrenders unconditionally" and threatened Tehran's power plants (Yahoo, Al Jazeera, TradingEconomics attributing the oil move to "ceasefire hopes"). Note the campaign did **not** stop — the nightly strikes continued through night 13 (below); what is reported paused is the step-change, not the war. Treat the breakthroughs as REPORTED, not signed — the 17 June memorandum also "broke through" and then collapsed within two weeks. The simultaneity of maximum-pressure rhetoric and a live back-channel is not a contradiction; it is a single coercive-diplomacy strategy, and today the market chose the diplomacy leg. Reversibility is violent in both directions: if the reports prove hollow, Brent snaps back through $100; a genuine signature fades it toward Goldman's $80 base case.

### The nuclear firebreak held a fifth straight window — night 13 spared the enrichment complex even as the bombers that could break it sit in theatre.
The US completed its **13th consecutive night of strikes** (CENTCOM), hitting military command centres, drone-storage, communications, coastal-surveillance and maritime targets across Khorramabad, Jask, Ahvaz and Bandar Abbas, with at least four killed per Iranian state media. What was *not* hit is the signal: **Fordow, Natanz, Esfahan, Pickaxe Mountain and the Bushehr reactor remain untouched** for a fifth consecutive window, even as Trump threatens Tehran's power grid and demands total surrender. Iran's own restraint is intact — its negotiators reasserted that enrichment "is our right… not negotiable" and that Iran is "prepared to stand up to a ground invasion," but that is a claim of *right* and a bracing posture, not an escalation step: no NPT-withdrawal move, no announced enrichment increase. The war's defining firebreak — neither side crossing the nuclear threshold — is now its most durable feature, and it is holding on both sides at once.

### The physical shipping market is pricing maximal disruption while the oil future prices resolution — the cleanest divergence of the cycle, and the hard print finally arrived.
For weeks this brief has flagged that the war-risk-insurance cascade rested on a commentator's number the desk rejected. That number has now been replaced by hard operational data: the **VLCC benchmark rate (Middle East to China) hit an all-time high of $423,736 per day** (LSEG), major marine war-risk providers are **scrapping cover for the Persian Gulf entirely**, vessels with US, UK or Israeli association pay triple, and Saudi-port cover repriced within 24 hours of the tanker strikes (Lloyd's List). Separately, **QatarEnergy declared force majeure** on long-term LNG contracts, taking roughly 20% of global LNG trade offline and driving European TTF gas to a four-month high above €61/MWh — the realized disruption the ECB explicitly cited in pausing on Wednesday. The tell is the split-screen: on the very day Brent crashed 8% on ceasefire hopes, the insurance and tanker complex priced the realized disruption at its most extreme. Both cannot hold for long — the future is pricing the probability of resolution; the physical market is pricing the disruption that already exists.

### The US struck the Houthis — a second kinetic front — but Saudi Arabia, given a US "leeway," has still not retaliated for its burning tankers.
Following Tuesday's Houthi missile-and-drone strike on the Saudi tankers *Encelia* and *Layla*, **US forces struck Houthi missile sites in Yemen** (confirmed by US officials via Fox and Yahoo) — the first American strike on the Houthis this cycle, opening a second front alongside the nightly Iran campaign, with Trump vowing "major military punishment" and to "hold Iran responsible." The restraint to watch is Riyadh's: Saudi Arabia is **"mulling" military escalation** with what its defence minister frames as US "leeway" to hit the Houthis, but it has **not militarily retaliated** for the tanker strikes, and Middle East Eye reports a royal-court split over how hard to respond. The forward risk is the Houthi threat to hit "all Saudi oil facilities" if Riyadh strikes Yemen — which would put Saudi *production* infrastructure, a materially higher rung than transiting hulls, into play. For now the discipline holds: the US acted, Saudi Arabia has not.

### India's decline stayed orderly — and the tariff "cliff" the market feared today is actually eight days away.
A correction to the calendar the reader should carry: the hard US reciprocal-tariff deadline for India is **1 August, not 24 July** — Washington extended the suspension from 9 July to 1 August, and Trump says it "will not be extended," with the interim first-tranche deal targeted for the autumn (Deccan Herald). Today is the RBI's weekly reserves print and a negotiation waypoint, not the cliff. On the tape the war and tariffs kept the Nifty down a fourth straight day, closing near **23,869 (−0.53%)**, below 24,000, with the **rupee resilient at ~₹96.45–96.6**, testing but not decisively breaking its record. The 200% generic-pharma tariff that led yesterday's domestic story is confirmed as *phased with a two-year grace* — zero duty until August, then 100% in 2028 and 200% in 2029 — so the near-term earnings hit is limited and the damage is structural, not immediate. The rupee held, the decline is orderly, and the genuine event risk sits at month-end, not today.

### Two of the day's biggest market moves are not the war at all — a defence-earnings pop and an AI-chip rout.
Worth isolating so the reader does not misattribute them. **Lockheed Martin jumped 10.5%** and RTX 7% — not on a fresh escalation but on a **Q2 earnings beat-and-raise** ($7.94 EPS versus $7.23 expected, a record backlog, a missile-production ramp); roughly two-thirds of the move is earnings execution, one-third the war-driven backlog, and none of it reverses on a ceasefire. In the opposite direction, **the KOSPI fell 5.7%, Taiwan 2.7% and the Nasdaq 2.2%** on a global semiconductor-valuation rout (Meta's AI-capex pivot, Samsung, SK Hynix, TSMC) that has erased roughly a third of the chip index's 2026 gain — an AI/memory-demand story, ~95% unrelated to the crisis, and notably *not* a vindication of the helium-supply thesis, which remains unconfirmed by any actual fab cut.
